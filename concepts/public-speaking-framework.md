# Public Speaking Framework

The **public-speaking framework** in the local source is Patrick Winston's source-described “How to Speak” lecture method: make the speaker visually present, simplify visual material, connect empathetically, and leave the audience with a memorable ending. It is a reusable presentation framework, not a verified causal explanation of speaking success. ^[raw/articles/thread-lumenxbt-2083603666603528596.md]

## The napkin framework

The thread says Winston's entire framework fits on a napkin:

1. **Do not read.** The source gives no further mechanics for what replaces reading notes or slides.
2. **Be in the image.** The source states this phrase without specifying a camera, slide layout, or staging setup.
3. **Keep images simple.**
4. **Eliminate clutter.**
5. **Start with an empathetic connection.**
6. **End with a punch line the audience can repeat over dinner.**
7. **Never open with a joke.**
8. **Never end with “thank you.”** ^[raw/articles/thread-lumenxbt-2083603666603528596.md]

The final two rules distinguish the framework from generic advice to be entertaining: the opening should not rely on a joke, and the closing should give the audience a repeatable line rather than a conventional “thank you.” The local source does not provide a sample slide deck, speech transcript, rehearsal procedure, timing, or evaluation rubric. ^[raw/articles/thread-lumenxbt-2083603666603528596.md]

## Lecture context

The source says Winston ran the MIT Artificial Intelligence Laboratory from 1972 to 1997, wrote the AI textbook “every computer science major in the world read for thirty years,” and gave “How to Speak” every January for four decades. It says he filmed the lecture once in January 2018 and died eighteen months later, then states that he died in 2019. These are source-described claims, not independently checked biographical findings. ^[raw/articles/thread-lumenxbt-2083603666603528596.md]

The thread quotes the lecture's opening line:

> “Your success in life will be determined largely by your ability to speak, your ability to write, and the quality of your ideas. In that order.” ^[raw/articles/thread-lumenxbt-2083603666603528596.md]

It says the lecture is free on MIT OpenCourseWare and the textbook is free on Winston's page. The local export contains no lecture or textbook URL, so the destinations were not resolved or fetched. ^[raw/articles/thread-lumenxbt-2083603666603528596.md]

## Source-framed reach and industry claims

The thread says ten million people watched the lecture, executive coaches charge $15,000 per session to teach a third of what Winston covered in one free hour, and the final rule alone may have cost executive coaching $100 million. It also says founders spend $80,000 on an MBA and then hire communications coaches for the same material, while engineers can lose promotions to teammates who watched the lecture. These numbers and comparisons are preserved as promotional or rhetorical source claims, not verified measurements. ^[raw/articles/thread-lumenxbt-2083603666603528596.md]

The source's practical conclusion is that almost none of the ten million viewers implemented the four rules, and that the edge is using the free framework in the next meeting. The source does not provide an implementation study, viewer sample, coaching-market analysis, or evidence that the lecture caused any industry effect. ^[raw/articles/thread-lumenxbt-2083603666603528596.md]
