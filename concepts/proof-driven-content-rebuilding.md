# Proof-Driven Content Rebuilding

**Proof-driven content rebuilding** is a source-described content system: find older posts with demonstrated engagement, identify the emotional mechanism that made them resonate, rebuild the underlying idea with a different angle, and connect the resulting emotion to a relevant offer. It treats viral content as market evidence rather than as text to copy.

## The four-step system

### 1. Find proof

The source's discovery filter is:

- posted more than 90 days ago;
- more than 5,000 likes;
- from the same or an adjacent niche; and
- strong enough to enter a running spreadsheet with the account, text, date, likes, niche relevance, and angle.

The source uses X advanced search, niche accounts with 50,000+ followers, and TweetDeck. It claims to have accumulated 340 candidate posts. These thresholds and the spreadsheet count are **source-described operating rules**, not independently validated platform-wide cutoffs.

### 2. Identify the resonance mode

The source reduces the reason a post worked to four modes:

| Mode | Reader response | What a rebuild must preserve |
|---|---|---|
| **Understood** | The reader feels a previously unarticulated experience was seen. | Recognition and relief. |
| **Smart** | The reader feels they gained a useful framework or insight. | Perceived explanatory value. |
| **Warned** | The reader sees a mistake or danger they may face. | Stakes and personal relevance. |
| **Access** | The reader receives insider information, numbers, or a process. | The feeling of privileged knowledge. |

The mode is treated as the mechanism, not as the post's wording or style. The operator can change the niche, details, structure, and length, but should preserve the mode that generated the original signal.

### 3. Rebuild with a different angle

The five source-described transformations are:

1. **Niche transfer** — move the same underlying insight into another niche.
2. **Inversion** — argue the opposite of the original central claim.
3. **Specificity upgrade** — replace vague outcomes with concrete numbers or circumstances.
4. **Before/after flip** — tell the same journey from the opposite temporal starting point.
5. **Mechanism reveal** — explain why the reported result happens instead of only stating the result.

The constraint is to change the expression while keeping the resonance mechanism legible. Whether this is sufficiently distinct from copying is the author's framing and is not a legal conclusion.

### 4. Bridge the resonance to the offer

The CTA should extend the feeling the post created rather than append a generic “link in bio.” The source's mapping is:

- **Understood** → offer a system that helps the reader avoid or solve the described situation.
- **Smart** → offer the full framework and worked examples.
- **Warned** → offer a checklist for the risk.
- **Access** → offer the complete breakdown or process.

The CTA is therefore a conversion bridge between attention and the relevant [[faceless-digital-product-portfolio]] asset.

## Operating cadence

The source reports running three pages, each posting three times daily: one original post and two rebuilt posts. The original stream supplies fresh experiments and candidate material; the rebuilt stream is presented as the reliable revenue driver. Its weekly routine is:

| Day | Work | Reported time |
|---|---|---:|
| Monday | Add 5–10 proven posts to the spreadsheet. | 30 min |
| Tuesday | Select six posts and classify their resonance modes. | 20 min |
| Wednesday | Rebuild the six with different angles, using [[claude]] for draft assistance. | 90 min |
| Thursday | Write resonance-matched CTAs. | 20 min |
| Friday | Schedule the six posts across three pages. | 30 min |

The claimed total is under 3.5 hours per week for six pieces across three pages. The reported engagement and revenue split — 187 average likes for rebuilt posts versus 34 for originals, and 78% of eight-month revenue attributed to rebuilt content — is **source-claimed**, not independently audited.
