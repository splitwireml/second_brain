# UGC (User-Generated Content)

User-generated content created by individuals rather than brands. In AI marketing context, UGC refers to authentic-seeming content created at scale using AI avatars and characters. Drives organic reach through algorithm-preferred authentic formats.

## Conversion-first human-creator variant (Matt, October 2026)

[[mattgittleson]] uses UGC to mean a creator running a dedicated product account: it looks/posts like an organic user and the product appears in every video. This is the source's human-creator variant, not an assumed AI-avatar production stack. At [[hardlaunch]] he says the same system used for his own app and [[jenni-ai]] scales from one phone/account to a thousand; every video must buy installs, web sign-ups, revenue or mindshare, never only views. His copy → adapt → propagate ladder means copying a working format first, adapting its mechanism rather than script, then repeating the winner across accounts while it has life. He says copy alone has taken apps to $20k MRR but caps out. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-i-went-from-filming-ugc-in-my-room-to-14b-views-in-2107530746923823444.md]

### Stage 1 — choose distribution and a viral format before building

Matt rejects the conventional painful-problem → speak to 100 potential users → build → market sequence, arguing that day-one distribution potential sets the ceiling. His alternative is format/distribution first, then the minimum app needed to deliver the format. For a reveal format, the reveal must be excellent; do not build features absent from the video. He says an evening checking whether the niche has content that does numbers can save six months, and that some products cannot be fixed by downstream marketing. This is his product-selection position, not a universal invalidation of problem validation. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-i-went-from-filming-ugc-in-my-room-to-14b-views-in-2107530746923823444.md]

His discovery sources, ranked worst to best, are (3) direct competitors, useful for the first fortnight but crowded; (2) unrelated app categories, dropshippers, course sellers and ecom brands, borrowing a scroll-stopping mechanism and applying it where LTV is better; (1) non-selling content with genuinely organic cultural resonance. His reported best format of the year came from a relationship video with no product/promo, adapted around an unrelated client's product; its first video passed 60M views. His prescribed research is **30 minutes a day inside the niche plus 30 minutes outside**: product precedents inside, culture/next formats outside. He calls this the highest-leverage hour of his day for two years. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-i-went-from-filming-ugc-in-my-room-to-14b-views-in-2107530746923823444.md]

Four selection criteria must all hold: **viral right now** (not speculative or from three years ago); **scalable to 50 variations** without staleness; **survived a fatigue cycle** (hit, dip, return rather than only rising); **can carry the product on screen**, either a demo or at least name/logo. He recommends scrolling TikTok rather than Reels, claiming Reels runs about two weeks behind and loses the early-mover advantage. These platform/timing claims are source-described, not independently verified. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-i-went-from-filming-ugc-in-my-room-to-14b-views-in-2107530746923823444.md]

His taste rules: never let AI write scripts, especially hooks (he says even dozens of human examples yield slightly off output, unlike a 19-year-old describing a specific problem/scenario); never let creators pick formats (he says visible/payable views push them toward easy filming and high reach); prescribe formats and hooks, let creators own execution; delegate filming/editing/posting before content strategy. He claims a frontier 10% taste advantage and 10% better strategy yields 10x agency views; these are his judgments. See [[mobile-app-organic-virality]] for the product-design variant. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-i-went-from-filming-ugc-in-my-room-to-14b-views-in-2107530746923823444.md]

### Stage 2 — run one account yourself, then warm and hire

Start with one account and one creator, the founder: learn to create formats before briefing others and learn bad execution before judging creators. Matt contrasts costly influencers and paid CPMs that can exceed $10 with high-volume UGC under $0.30 CPM, and says credible natural recommendation supports conversion. These cost/conversion assertions are his, not established platform rates. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-i-went-from-filming-ugc-in-my-room-to-14b-views-in-2107530746923823444.md]

His days 1–4 protocol is 15–30 minutes/day using the app like a human: niche scrolling, liking and actually finishing videos. He claims completed watch time teaches the algorithm more than swipes. On Instagram follow small likely-to-follow-back niche accounts; at Jenni creators had to reach 40 followers before promotion. Days 4–7 are three days of quick, high-viral-potential warmup posts with no selling, no product and not the campaign format. The overlapping day 4 boundary is kept exactly as described. He reports two warmup videos followed by the creator's first two promotional videos totaling over 7.5M views on a brand-new campaign account, especially treating IG warmups as a diagnostic. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-i-went-from-filming-ugc-in-my-room-to-14b-views-in-2107530746923823444.md]

He distinguishes **zero/single-digit views** (almost never content: unwarmed, flagged or shadowbanned account) from **a few hundred consistently** (content issue: the account reached FYP but viewers swiped). Do not rewrite a good format for an account issue, in his framing. This is a heuristic, not a verified diagnosis or guarantee. Full warmup details remain distinct from other sources in [[tiktok-account-infrastructure]]. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-i-went-from-filming-ugc-in-my-room-to-14b-views-in-2107530746923823444.md]

Hire for the **ICP**, not niche-reviewer status: visibly credible students filming in bedrooms at 1am before deadlines for a study app, moms for a mom ICP, or someone visibly in a professional job. He says mismatched creators hurt retention and CTA trust. Sometimes use the **recommender ICP**, such as a teacher recommending a study app or one mom recommending to another; the person on screen must be credible to the viewer. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-i-went-from-filming-ugc-in-my-room-to-14b-views-in-2107530746923823444.md]

### Stage 3 — product purpose, hook test, and comment conversion

The **hook and product must be the same idea**, not adjacent: whatever the hook promises, the product delivers. His construction is **Problem → promised relief → your product is the mechanism**. Examples: ‘Here's how I FINALLY made my IG pics look aesthetic’ and ‘STOP deleting your horizontal pics’: the answer/payoff is the app, not an interruption. His pre-ship removal test asks: **if you removed the product entirely, would the video still make sense?** If yes, he calls the product decoration and predicts zero conversion. That categorical prediction remains a source claim. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-i-went-from-filming-ugc-in-my-room-to-14b-views-in-2107530746923823444.md]

His failure examples are (a) three anonymous, uncut videos celebrated on X that neither show nor name the app, which he says created views but no conversion, mindshare or brand awareness; (b) a product-free 1M-view video under a hypothetical $1 CPM bonus costing $1,000 for $0 revenue; and (c) a successful talking-head story adding the unrelated app around second 67, losing the viewer or their interest. His own early 100k-view success showed no signup/trial in Stripe or RevenueCat after repeated 200/300-view posts. He says unit economics, not organic-looking reach alone, decide whether the format wastes money. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-i-went-from-filming-ugc-in-my-room-to-14b-views-in-2107530746923823444.md]

His positive examples: a product-purpose video above 15M views; another campaign with **350,000 extra downloads in the first four weeks at 7-cent CPI**, described as measured rather than assumed with no paid spend; and Jenni's **$10,000 attributed revenue** from one video versus a **different video's $4k MRR and about $25,000 lifetime revenue**. They remain distinct reported outcomes; the local source supplies no analytics export, attribution implementation, or controls. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-i-went-from-filming-ugc-in-my-room-to-14b-views-in-2107530746923823444.md]

The comment section is his second half of the job because he says viewers open it and the top comment does CTA/social-proof work. His protocol:

- Seed once the video crosses **10–20k views**; earlier is wasted effort, later gives comments time to collect likes and climb.
- Give intent/principles, not scripts: lowercase, informal, occasional typo; he says scripted wording is detectable.
- Seed from a personal account, not only the campaign account; mix those with campaign accounts. He claims a non-brand-looking account reads as a real person.
- Catch and answer **‘what app is this?’**, his high-intent signal.
- Distinguish argumentative comments he considers useful engagement from flat-out negativity, and remove the bad ones ASAP.

These are source-described seeding/moderation practices; no comment automation tool, sample scripts, engagement-service provider, or measured lift is supplied. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-i-went-from-filming-ugc-in-my-room-to-14b-views-in-2107530746923823444.md]

### Stage 4 — iterate, propagate, diversify, delegate

Review every creator's drafts and message them daily. Change one variable per additive experiment (hook text, opening line, visual wrapper, ending twist), preserve the central viral mechanism, and validate across different accounts and faces: one person's hit may be a strong creator, not a repeatable format. Do not spam the identical winner across all accounts simultaneously; Matt says this shortens life from three months to a fortnight and concentrates risk. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-i-went-from-filming-ugc-in-my-room-to-14b-views-in-2107530746923823444.md]

The format portfolio is **50% proven formats / 25% iterations / 25% moonshots**: a dependable floor, one-variable improvement bets, and a replacement pipeline despite most novel bets dying. Avoid 100% reliance on proven formats and avoid one account supplying most views; **50% of campaign views from one account** is his shadowban single-point-of-failure example. Monitor health and rotate underperforming creators. His reported 60M original was followed within 30 days by two videos above 20M, nine above 5M, thirty-four above 1M, and follow-ups above 100M; do not infer non-overlapping buckets. See [[format-propagation]] for the full protocol. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-i-went-from-filming-ugc-in-my-room-to-14b-views-in-2107530746923823444.md]

Retain the founder's **20% upfront taste/direction and 5% final taste pass**, delegate **75% briefing, filming, editing, scheduling and daily creator management**; Matt says he keeps both ends for every campaign. He describes boring execution: warming a week, scrolling an hour daily, making the twenty-sixth version of a May winner, or patiently making the same video 45 times. These are discipline examples, not tested numerical success thresholds. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-i-went-from-filming-ugc-in-my-room-to-14b-views-in-2107530746923823444.md]

## Evidence boundaries for the October guide

The headline **1.4B views in 90 days**, **$1M+ MRR**, **zero paid spend**, **7¢ CPI**, **<$0.30 CPM**, and third-party-counted views are source descriptions; the unnamed analytics platform and underlying data are unavailable locally. His own app's **$0** self-filming, **$375,000 sale after six months**, and **30M views** are reported anecdotes. He estimates equivalent paid reach north of **fourteen million dollars**, says organic posts still draw views/installs months later while paid stops with spend, and predicts rising CPMs/format fatigue and category ownership in the next twelve months. These comparisons/forecasts are not verified rates or outcomes. No named AI model, version, prompt pipeline, scheduling interface, file format, management tool or instrumentation stack is supplied; Stripe and RevenueCat are named dashboard checks, not an invented end-to-end analytics integration. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-i-went-from-filming-ugc-in-my-room-to-14b-views-in-2107530746923823444.md]
