# Five-Stage Distribution Roadmap

The **five-stage distribution roadmap** is [[whotfiszackk]]'s source-described sequence for building customer acquisition as a system rather than adding promotion after product development:

```text
Foundation → Community → Owned audience → Search → Multiplication
```

The framework begins with one targeting question: **who is the specific person with the problem, and where do they go when that problem is most acute?** The source argues that targeting this moment determines the communities, search terms, free resource, email sequence, and partners used downstream. This is a concrete implementation of [[distribution]], not evidence that every channel or timeline will perform as claimed.

## System interfaces

```text
Acute-pain buyer definition
→ positioning + proof + free resource
→ community trust and search discovery
→ free-resource opt-in
→ owned email relationship
→ offer
→ customer results
→ referrals, partnerships, and recombined content
```

Every public channel hands interested people to the free resource; the free resource captures email; the email sequence hands qualified interest to the offer. Proof sits one click from the landing surface. Customer results feed the referral and partnership layer, while successful material is reused across community, email, search, and partner channels.

## Stage 1 — Foundation

Build this before publishing distribution content:

1. **Acute-pain buyer definition:** replace a broad category such as “founders” with a narrow operating situation, revenue stage, current workaround, and felt consequence.
2. **Positioning statement:** one sentence naming exactly who the product serves, the problem it solves, and why it is better than the current workaround. It becomes the through-line for community posts, email, search content, and partnership pitches.
3. **Proof document:** collect raw testimonials, result screenshots, before/after case studies, and specific numbers. Keep proof credible and one click from the customer's landing point rather than hiding it behind polished copy or multiple navigation steps.
4. **Free resource:** completely solve one bounded version of the problem in one sitting with a working checklist, template, or mini-guide. The source treats this as the first product experience, not a vague teaser.

**Failure mode:** starting distribution without coherent positioning, accessible proof, or a useful first experience. The channels then send inconsistent signals and fail to accumulate recognition.

## Stage 2 — Community

1. Identify the five online places where the acute-pain conversation already occurs—not merely broad or adjacent communities.
2. Concentrate on the two or three with the strongest activity and buyer match.
3. For the first four weeks, contribute without promoting: answer neglected questions, add missing specifics, and move conversations from frustration to informed action.
4. Maintain roughly two or three meaningful contributions per day across the selected communities.
5. Introduce the product in week five only after the name has become associated with useful answers.

The source gives example surfaces such as LinkedIn comment sections, founder Slack groups, niche subreddits, specialist Facebook groups, association forums, product communities, and relevant X threads. Its claimed curve is a trickle in month one, consistency by month three, and a reputation-supported channel by month six.

**Failure mode:** leading with the product before reputation exists. The sequencing rule is explicit: **reputation first, product second**. The conversion examples and month-by-month curve are source claims, not verified benchmarks.

## Stage 3 — Owned audience

Community trust exists in “someone else's house”; the email list is the direct channel intended to reduce platform dependence and retain subscriber history.

### Capture handoff

- Community: offer the relevant checklist or template when it directly answers the discussion.
- Search: place the full template/resource behind the opt-in.
- Social: direct the profile or post CTA to the resource.
- Resource: capture email, then begin the sequence.

### Five-email trust sequence

| Email | Timing | Purpose |
|---|---:|---|
| 1 | Immediately | Deliver the resource and set one expectation for what follows. |
| 2 | Day 2 | Add useful material that builds on the resource; no pitch. |
| 3 | Day 4 | Show a specific same-situation case study and the full-product result. |
| 4 | Day 6 | Address the most common obstacle to acting; no direct pitch. |
| 5 | Day 8 | Present the offer as the next step for someone who benefited from the resource. |

After the sequence, the source recommends **two to three worthwhile emails per week**—not daily, but not monthly—so the sender remains familiar.

**Failure mode:** collecting subscribers and rarely mailing them. A dormant list is treated as forgotten access, not an owned distribution asset. Deliverability, consent mechanics, segmentation implementation, and tooling are not specified in the source.

## Stage 4 — Search

1. Find the exact question the ideal customer searches when the pain is acute.
2. Write the most useful and specific answer available—not the longest article or a keyword-stuffed page.
3. Publish it on an indexable page.
4. Repeat for the twelve most common acute-pain questions.
5. Allow the source's stated **two-to-four-month** window before judging the channel.

The article illustrates the model with four hours per article, 3,000 monthly searches, 90 monthly visitors, a 3% conversion rate, and three customers per article; twelve articles therefore yield a claimed 36 customers per month. Those inputs and outputs are illustrative source math, not independently validated forecasts.

**Failure mode:** optimizing for search engines instead of usefulness. The source assumes useful content will retain rankings, but does not specify keyword-research tools, competition analysis, link acquisition, technical SEO, attribution, or conversion instrumentation.

## Stage 5 — Multiplication

### Referrals

After the first 30 customers have had enough time to get a result, identify the 10 who showed the strongest satisfaction through reviews, replies, or public mentions. Send each a personal—not automated—message:

> “you mentioned this changed how you approach X. that genuinely means a lot. i wanted to ask — do you know one or two other people dealing with the same situation? i'd love to take care of them at a discount if you want to pass my name along.”

The mechanism is transferred trust: the referred buyer arrives through someone who has experienced the result. The source does not provide measured referral rates.

### Partnerships

Identify 10 accounts, newsletters, podcasts, or communities already serving the **exact** audience. Offer one of:

- free product access so the partner can form an honest opinion;
- revenue share that makes the introduction financially worthwhile;
- a co-created resource that is exclusive to the partner's audience.

The source claims one exact-audience partnership can outperform six months of organic social growth; this is an unverified comparison, not a general benchmark.

### Content compounding

Treat every successful artifact as reusable raw material:

```text
Saved community post → forwarded email
High-open email → search article
Search article → partnership framework
Partnership pitch → partner-perspective community post
```

Nothing is intended to be created once and used once. This mechanism extends [[content-compounding-system]] from a principle into a cross-channel handoff rule.

## Six-month rollout

| Window | Work and expected handoff |
|---|---|
| Weeks 1–2 | Write positioning; collect proof; build the free resource; identify five communities. |
| Weeks 3–6 | Build community reputation without promoting; publish the first three search answers. |
| Weeks 7–10 | Introduce the free resource; capture email; launch the five-email sequence; publish three more search pieces; approach 10 potential partners. |
| Weeks 11–16 | Maintain community acquisition; watch early search rankings; advance a partnership; ask successful early customers for referrals. |
| Months 4–6 | Run community, email, search, referral, and partnership mechanisms together; reuse successful material across them. |
| Month 6+ | Maintain and manage the system rather than treating it as fantasy autopilot. |

The schedule is the author's proposed operating model. It is not verified for different markets, products, domain authority, list quality, or sales cycles.

## Source variant: faceless-page $100,000 compounding model

The 2026-08-04 local article reuses the foundation → community → owned audience → search → multiplication architecture but supplies a separate, more aggressive numeric model. It defines $100,000 as eight to twelve months of recurring revenue, not one viral post or launch. At $47 per product, the target is 177 customers/month: community 30–40, search 40–50 from 12 pieces, email 50–60 from 1,500 subscribers and a five-email sequence converting at 4%, and referral/partnership 30–40; the stated total is 150–190 customers and $7,050–$8,930/month. The article later describes partnerships as a fifth component, so its channel arithmetic should remain source-specific rather than silently reconciled.^[raw/articles/xarticle-if-youre-building-a-faceless-page-and-you-havent-h-2084658027219828755.md]

**Foundation and community.** Phase one takes two weeks: write the one-sentence positioning statement (the source contrasts 1% conversion for “might be interested” with 8% for “exactly right”), then build a free checklist, template, or mini-guide that works on its own in three days. It contrasts a 4% versus 0.8% email conversion on 1,500 subscribers (60 versus 12 customers, or $2,820 versus $564 at $47 per product). In weeks 3–6, identify five acute-pain communities—examples are a subreddit, Facebook group, LinkedIn comment section, or Discord server—and answer the urgent question daily for four weeks without mentioning the product; promotion starts in week seven. The source claims trusted recommendations convert at six times cold promotion and the channel can deliver 30–40 customers/month.^[raw/articles/xarticle-if-youre-building-a-faceless-page-and-you-havent-h-2084658027219828755.md]

**Search and owned email.** Weeks 3–12 run search simultaneously with community: mine repeated unanswered questions from those communities, write the most useful answer to 12 of them, and publish each on an indexable page. The source claims 40–50 customers/month, persistent results through month 24, and no extra effort after initial writing; it names Google search but no SEO tool or ranking method. Starting week seven, every channel points to the free resource and its email address. The five-email sequence is: immediate delivery plus one sentence about what follows; useful follow-up with no pitch; a same-situation case study; an honest discussion of the common action barrier; and the offer as the logical next step. The source claims 40–60 new subscribers/week, 1,500 by month six, 2,400 by month nine, and 3,600 by month twelve; at 4%, the last figure is 144 customers or $6,768 per Thursday.^[raw/articles/xarticle-if-youre-building-a-faceless-page-and-you-havent-h-2084658027219828755.md]

**Multiplication and timeline.** After the first 30 customers get results, the source asks the first 30 happy customers by direct personal message (not an automated notification) for referrals and claims 18% referred conversion. From month three it proposes 10 adjacent newsletter operators/community managers, with 2–3 accepting and 20–30 customers per event; three quarterly partnerships are claimed as 60–90 customers. The timeline claims $1,500–$3,000/month in months 1–2, $4,000–$6,000 in months 3–4, $7,000–$10,000 in months 5–6, and $8,000–$15,000 in months 7–12, totaling $85,000–$120,000. It gives no provider, CRM, analytics/attribution, deliverability or consent mechanics, technical SEO, paid-acquisition plan, or channel stop condition; all rates, customer counts, and revenue are source claims.^[raw/articles/xarticle-if-youre-building-a-faceless-page-and-you-havent-h-2084658027219828755.md]

The referral script is preserved as source-specific copy: “you mentioned the checklist caught something you would have missed. i wanted to ask — do you know one or two people dealing with the same situation? i'd love to take care of them at a discount if you wanted to pass it along.”

## Boundaries and omissions

The source specifies stages, channel handoffs, cadences, scripts, and timing, but does **not** specify:

- paid acquisition or when to add it;
- CRM, email-service, analytics, attribution, or automation tools;
- deliverability, consent, unsubscribe, or platform-policy implementation;
- search competition, backlink, and technical-SEO methods;
- channel-specific conversion baselines or stop conditions;
- how to choose between channels when resources cannot support all five.

These gaps matter when converting the roadmap into an instrumented operating plan. They do not erase the framework's central distinction: distribution is developed alongside the product rather than deferred until launch.

## Evidence layers

- **Confirmed:** Bird returned the complete 22,030-character X Article, preserved verbatim at `raw/articles/xarticle-the-full-distribution-roadmap-2081513372617765197.md`; the source explicitly defines the five stages, handoffs, cadences, referral script, and rollout timeline above.
- **Source-claimed:** community conversion examples, month-by-month channel maturity, email conversion superiority, search-volume/conversion math, partnership leverage, and six-month system behavior.
- **Likely:** coherent targeting, proof, owned follow-up, and cross-channel reuse reduce waste compared with disconnected promotion.
- **Speculative:** that all five channels will compound on the proposed schedule or produce customers with only fractional maintenance after month six.
