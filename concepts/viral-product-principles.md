# Viral Product Principles

A landing-page and launch-positioning framework from [[marc-lou]] that argues viral products win through radical simplicity, visible pricing, emotional copy, and clear one-step conversion paths.

## Core Thesis

The post compresses product marketing into 32 short heuristics. The repeated pattern is that memorability beats completeness: one idea per screen, one call to action, one obvious product promise, and pricing that reduces decision load instead of increasing it.

## Main Clusters

### Simplicity
- Use three colors at most
- Reveal one idea per screen
- Keep the product description under 10 words
- Build a product known for one thing

### Conversion
- Put a hard paywall in front of non-serious users
- Make pricing impossible to miss
- Prefer three pricing tiers ("Good, Better, Best")
- Use a CTA that describes the next action exactly

### Copy and Positioning
- Prefer numbers over vague adjectives
- Write headlines simple enough for a fifth grader
- Use customer language instead of founder language
- Sell outcomes and desire, not features

### Social Proof and Virality
- End pages with a share-worthy footer
- Treat the OG image like a thumbnail
- Launch with testimonials already collected
- Surprise people with something they have not seen before

## Practical Tension

Some principles are strong opinions rather than universal rules. For example, rejecting free plans and subscriptions may fit bootstrapped solo-SaaS economics, but they should be treated as source-specific heuristics rather than general product law.

## Why It Matters

This page is useful as a compact checklist for launch pages and distribution-heavy products, especially where the bottleneck is attention and conversion rather than feature depth. It complements [[viral-marketing]] and [[landing-page-ai-workflow]] by focusing less on traffic generation and more on what the page itself should communicate once traffic arrives.
