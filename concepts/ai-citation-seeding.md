## Definition

A distribution tactic that tries to increase citation likelihood in LLMs and Google AI Overviews by placing niche-relevant content across already-ranking third-party domains, then repeating brand/entity mentions across that visible SERP surface.

## Core playbook from the source

The source's operating sequence is:

1. Publish or syndicate content on high-authority domains such as Reddit, Quora, Medium, and other niche-relevant "parasite" surfaces.
2. Target valuable keywords directly in titles, descriptions, and paragraph text.
3. Mine FAQ questions in the niche and answer them across those external surfaces.
4. Repeat the brand name and site/entity references throughout the distributed content.
5. Where the operator controls the profile or page, link back to the main site.
6. For local-service variants, reverse-engineer whichever directories, review sites, and BBB-style properties already dominate the local SERP.

### Likely but not independently verified here
- That AI answer engines use an "average of top search results" strongly enough for this tactic to work as described.
- That tiered links and paid guest posts materially improve AI citation capture rather than mostly affecting traditional ranking.

## Why it matters

This tactic is useful to track because it sits at the edge between legitimate distribution, classic parasite SEO, and AI-answer-engine visibility. Even if some implementation details are spam-sensitive, the source captures a live operator belief: LLM visibility can be shaped by occupying more of the SERP surface, not only by improving the primary site.

## Risks and limits

- The source does not independently prove that citation uplift came from these tactics.
- Some recommendations, especially paid guest posts, aggressive brand seeding, and tiered links, may create search-quality or spam-policy risk.
- The thread is tactical, not methodological: it offers a playbook, not controlled evidence.
