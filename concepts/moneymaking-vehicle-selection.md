# Moneymaking Vehicle Selection

A decision framework for choosing how to make money based on personal fit rather than trend imitation. The core claim is that many business models can work, but the highest-probability path depends on whether the vehicle matches a person's interests, strengths, dislikes, risk tolerance, desired income, and available time or capital.

## Core claims

- **No universal best path**: advice that claims one best way to make money is usually over-generalized, trend-driven, or self-serving.
- **Personal fit first**: the first filter is whether the work is interesting enough to sustain repetition, skill-building, and setbacks.
- **Eliminate bad fits early**: list traits, likes, and dislikes, then rule out vehicles that structurally conflict with them.
- **Reverse-engineer the target**: pick a monthly income goal, adjust for taxes or operating costs, define an offer, then compute the number of customers and the amount of outreach or output required.
- **Execution beats over-planning**: the initial research and decision phase should be short; learning compounds once real work begins.

## Example operating logic from the source

The article walks through a hypothetical email-marketing agency: choose a monthly net target, translate that into gross revenue, price an offer, calculate how many clients are needed, then derive how many outreach actions are required from the observed close rate. This turns an abstract wealth goal into a concrete activity requirement.

## Practical takeaway

The framework is less about discovering a hidden opportunity and more about choosing a vehicle that you can persist in long enough to become excellent. It favors self-knowledge, simple unit economics, and immediate action over trend-chasing or endless research.
