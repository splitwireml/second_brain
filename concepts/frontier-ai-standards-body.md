# Frontier AI Standards Body

A proposed U.S.-initiated institution for dynamic testing, benchmarking, and safety governance of frontier AI models. The proposal comes from [[demis-hassabis]]'s article and is intended to combine technical rigor with continued innovation rather than impose a static approval regime. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-a-framework-for-frontier-ai-and-the-dawning-of-a-n-2076957440109625718.md]

## Institutional design

The article proposes a federally overseen public-private partnership or self-regulatory organization modeled on FINRA. Its board would include independent technical experts and open-source representatives, while substantial industry funding would provide the talent and compute needed for large-scale testing. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-a-framework-for-frontier-ai-and-the-dawning-of-a-n-2076957440109625718.md]

## Assessment model

- A model would qualify as **Frontier-class** when it crosses thresholds on a changing set of benchmarks; organizations building those models would become **Frontier Labs**.
- Frontier Labs would initially share models for review up to 30 days before release, with formal pre-deployment requirements potentially following once the protocol proved effective.
- Assessments would cover cybersecurity, biological threats, and other high-risk domains, plus agentic tests for guardrail bypass and deception.
- Best practices would include model cards, strong internal cybersecurity, personnel vetting, safety and security research, and post-release vulnerability work.
- Benchmarks would be refreshed regularly, with saturated tests deprecated, held-out evaluations developed independently of labs, and third-party auditors helping with assessment and benchmark development. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-a-framework-for-frontier-ai-and-the-dawning-of-a-n-2076957440109625718.md]

## Governance thesis

The framework is designed to be technically focused, adaptable as risks become clearer, and capable of ratcheting up requirements if circumstances demand it. The source also presents the U.S. effort as a possible starting point for shared international standards, while exempting non-frontier models from the process. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-a-framework-for-frontier-ai-and-the-dawning-of-a-n-2076957440109625718.md]

## Open questions

The proposal leaves unresolved how to define capability thresholds without rapid benchmark gaming, how to preserve institutional independence under industry funding, how formal enforcement would work, and how a U.S.-initiated system could reach international legitimacy. Those questions are part of the concept's design space rather than settled conclusions.
