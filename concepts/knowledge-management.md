# Knowledge Management

Systems and practices for capturing, organizing, and retrieving knowledge. Core for personal productivity ([[second-brain]], obsidian), team knowledge ([[company-brain]]), and agent memory.

## Goal-directed learning as a knowledge-management filter (2026-07-26)

Dan Koe's source argues that the common failure mode is treating learning as input: hoarding books, courses, spaced repetition, flashcards, and fundamentals without an output that makes the knowledge relevant. Its alternative is cybernetic: a meaningful goal supplies a **reference signal**, perception reads the current state, a comparator computes the gap/error signal, and behavior acts to reduce the error. In the source's compressed chain, **goal → error signal → filter → relevance → retention**. These are the source's claims, not a general empirical law established by this page. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-how-to-remember-everything-you-read-stop-trying-2081415714636996844.md]

The operational sequence is: (1) become obsessed with a meaningful goal by confronting where current actions lead; (2) treat the mind, body, relationships, and finances as projects with goals and milestones; (3) take the first step toward the project instead of "starting learning"; and (4) seek specific knowledge only when the project requires it. The source uses **After Effects** as an example of finding a tutorial close to the intended project and stacking one useful technique at a time, and guitar as an example of learning a chosen song chord by chord rather than studying music theory first. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-how-to-remember-everything-you-read-stop-trying-2081415714636996844.md]

## Digital commonplace book / second subconscious

The article distinguishes a collection that fuels creation from a note-taking archive that is never revisited. It cites [[dan-koe]]'s experience with **Roam Research**, his failed-but-evolved **Kortex** product, and the later **Eden** product context; it compares the digital commonplace-book idea with **Marcus Aurelius' _Meditations_**, **Leonardo da Vinci's** notebooks, and variations used by **Mark Twain, H.P. Lovecraft, Thomas Jefferson, Ronald Reagan, Montaigne, and Rick Rubin**. Its Seneca metaphor is to gather pollen from many flowers and digest it into one's own honey — "steal like an artist." ^[raw/articles/xarticle-how-to-remember-everything-you-read-stop-trying-2081415714636996844.md]

The source's capture/retrieval choices are:

- **Option 1:** [[claude-obsidian-second-brain-stack]] — Claude Code + Obsidian, with a `"save an idea"` skill, an inbox note with a clear title, a `"process my inbox"` skill that adds tags, routes notes, and adds backlinks, a Claude-assisted taxonomy, and later vault search.
- **Option 2:** **MyMind or [[eden]]** — a library that automatically tags, categorizes, and embeds ideas in "meaning space." Eden is described as saving Substack/X/YouTube sources, transcribing and highlighting them, importing Kindle highlights, offering an MCP for Claude, and providing an outlier search feed and scheduled posts.

The article says Eden represents each idea with approximately `~1500` numbers used as GPS-like coordinates, making meaning-based retrieval possible even with zero shared words. It contrasts that convenience with maintaining a vector database, an embedding API, indexing scripts, re-embedding when notes change, synchronization after files are renamed or deleted, and caching to avoid paying again for unchanged content. These implementation and product details remain source-described. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-how-to-remember-everything-you-read-stop-trying-2081415714636996844.md]

## Capture-to-creation loop

The source says curation matters more when AI can create anything: capture ideas that represent the mind you want to build, reject an endless social-media firehose, read curated authors and thinkers, and treat research as roughly `80%` of a writer/creator's job before synthesizing it for others. It closes with a production workflow for a Substack article, YouTube script, or X article: choose a topic at the intersection of **performance** (proven social content/outlier posts) and **excitement** (curiosity), then **braindump → outline → draft** using **Problem → Insight → Solution** as the general storytelling frame. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-how-to-remember-everything-you-read-stop-trying-2081415714636996844.md]

AI should remove friction rather than articulate the creator's beliefs: ask for possible structures, directions for what to write next, or research when something is unclear. The author allows direct copying for pure information such as explanations of **Greuter's 9 stages of ego development**, but says the writer decides what gets published and must refine it until it represents the intended values, ideas, and beliefs. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-how-to-remember-everything-you-read-stop-trying-2081415714636996844.md]
