# AI Productivity

Using AI to amplify personal and team productivity — automation of repetitive tasks, faster research, smarter scheduling. Core use case for [[claude-code]], [[hermes-agent]], and [[agent]] systems.

## Voice-first personal AI and accountability (Amir D, 2026-10-07)

[[starks-arq]]'s **“How to stop working in the next 10 years (the complete guide)”** targets three problems: daily AI use without reduced work hours, more ideas than execution, and pursuing a goal never consciously chosen. Its goal is work consisting of **speaking and thinking**: “stop working” means eliminating unwanted work, not doing nothing. A mentor asked “What do you want in life?” and then “Why?” twice; the author's initial wealth/buying-things-for-family answer gave way to this self-chosen goal. These are autobiographical source claims, not measured outcomes. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-how-to-stop-working-in-the-next-10-years-the-compl-2107846605999063322.md]

### Learning and instruction before automation

The author says he left school when ChatGPT launched, worked for founders in AI **for free**, and initially treated ChatGPT as a search engine, mentor, and library. **System prompts** turned it into a private teacher for **filmmaking, cinematography, storytelling, and AI's effects on society**; no actual teacher prompt is supplied. When image and video models appeared, the author describes **12 to 16 hours a day**, every day, **tens of thousands of dollars** spent on tools, essentially every dollar earned reinvested, and effort at **120%**. He reports learning through volume and trial and error rather than a framework or course: **instructing AI, talking to AI, and thinking in AI**. These effort/cost figures remain source-described. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-how-to-stop-working-in-the-next-10-years-the-compl-2107846605999063322.md]

The instruction skill is to imagine, think, speak, **structure the idea**, and translate it into the right instruction. The source then applies AI to compiled personal **data, routines, output, and results** for reflection; its claim of improvements that would otherwise take an entire team analyzing a person for months is unverified. It explicitly says **“we don't use Higgsfield or Lovable”** and instead use **foundation models directly**, tailored to their lives. This is a source-specific choice, not evidence that those products are ineffective or a specification of which foundation models/API are used. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-how-to-stop-working-in-the-next-10-years-the-compl-2107846605999063322.md]

### Voice capture → processing → structure → draft

The author says this article started as a **12-minute voice message**. The recoverable handoff is **voice message → processing → structure → first draft** (“I talk. It gets processed. Structured. Drafted.”). Named outputs are **articles, tweets, presentations, negotiations, films, commercials, marketing strategies, and ways to go viral**. Voice, not an app, dashboard, or prompt box, is framed as the final interface: **“Speech → everything.”** The confidence that a stopped recording will be processed and yield a first draft is said to free attention for the next article, video, or teaching idea; “that freedom is the real product.” See [[first-draft-writing-workflow]] for the writing-layer integration. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-how-to-stop-working-in-the-next-10-years-the-compl-2107846605999063322.md]

### Personal data and historical feedback

The advice is to **talk to AI** and connect **email, WhatsApp, notes, ideas, thoughts, and old chat history**. The concrete local-source menu instruction is **ChatGPT: Settings → Data controls → Export data**. This records the article's interface path, not a live product check or a complete import recipe. ChatGPT conversations are said to have been saved **since 2023**, described by the author as **“almost four years”** of ideas, plans, and problems. Feedback loops over that archive allegedly exposed problems still present that had already been solved **two years ago**; the missing pieces were consistency, reminders, and accountability rather than another idea. AI is framed as the **ultimate accountability partner**. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-how-to-stop-working-in-the-next-10-years-the-compl-2107846605999063322.md]

The source's questions are: **“Why don't my ideas get executed?” “What's the friction?” “Why do you keep falling back into the same loops?”** Its final checklist phrases the diagnostic as **“Where is the friction? Where do I keep looping?”** No executable feedback-loop design, reminder delivery channel, persistence architecture, or data export/import format is provided. Personal-history reflection here is an adjacent use case to [[personal-ai-agent-architecture]] and [[claude-obsidian-second-brain-stack]], **not evidence that this author uses their Claude Desktop/MCP, Claude Code, Obsidian, or memory-file stacks**. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-how-to-stop-working-in-the-next-10-years-the-compl-2107846605999063322.md]

### Seven-step starter workflow

1. **Ask yourself “why?” twice.** Know what you actually want before building it.
2. **Record one voice message a day** containing an idea, problem, or plan; let AI make the **first draft**.
3. **Connect your data:** email, **WhatsApp**, notes, and old chat history; the provided export route is **ChatGPT: Settings → Data controls → Export data**.
4. **Ask one diagnostic question:** “Why don't my ideas get executed? Where is the friction? Where do I keep looping?”
5. **Automate the service first:** emails, outreach, and admin.
6. **Watch what you do with the free time:** that is the real answer to “why?”
7. **When the technology jumps, start over.**

The conclusion asks **“What do you want?”**, asks the reader to ask why **twice**, and requests a reply with the **second answer**. This is the article's behavioral/operational sequence, not a proven universal route to stopping unwanted work within **10 years**. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-how-to-stop-working-in-the-next-10-years-the-compl-2107846605999063322.md]

### Claimed automation and performance

The author reports working less daily over the past few months after automating **most emails**, **sales outreach**, and **finding viral topics on the internet**. In the KitKat anecdote, a loss of **12 tons of chocolate** became a meme within minutes; the team allegedly made a **KitKat video in 20 minutes** watched by **1.5 million people**. An agent is now said to spot similar moments at **10,000 impressions**. Free time allegedly began at **three hours a day**, and **four to five hours** of full-force work now completes what previously took **two or three days**. None of the event, reach, detection threshold, work comparison, or causal attribution was independently verified. The source provides no monitoring query, detection algorithm, video toolchain, performance logs, or comparison methodology. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-how-to-stop-working-in-the-next-10-years-the-compl-2107846605999063322.md]

When free time appeared, the author says the team returned to **telling stories**, treating what one does without external work pressure as evidence of what one wants. Earning from what one would do for free is the stated **next 10 years** goal. This connects automation to personally chosen activity rather than assuming AI usage alone creates meaningful leisure. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-how-to-stop-working-in-the-next-10-years-the-compl-2107846605999063322.md]

### AGI, adoption, and implementation boundaries

**Source opinion, not a wiki finding:** the author declares **“AGI is already here. For the individual.”**, claims **“We have entered the singularity”**, and suggests training **a model, a system, even an open-source model** on all personal data “the right way” can at least fix one's current life by automating simple tasks/service. “Train” is underspecified: the article does **not** establish fine-tuning versus prompting, retrieval, or another form of customization, and gives no objective AGI test. It contrasts individuals with enterprise frameworks and outdated laws and attributes a **95%** no-measurable-return figure for organizations' generative-AI initiatives to **MIT**, without a study title, link, methodology, or verification in this ingest. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-how-to-stop-working-in-the-next-10-years-the-compl-2107846605999063322.md]

The **“Ban the interface”** section criticizes fixed interfaces built for other people and urges early users to build what fits their lives. It names **Jensen Huang, Elon Musk, and Dario Amodei** as interview examples whose ordinary-American AI answers allegedly get too complicated, contrasting that with the simple example **“You can plan your entire trip with it.”** It attributes **about two in three American adults** not paying for any AI product to **Menlo Ventures' latest numbers** and says everyone in the author's house has an intelligence ally in their phone. The interview characterization, adoption statistic, household outcome, individual AGI, and singularity claims remain source-attributed, not independently established. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-how-to-stop-working-in-the-next-10-years-the-compl-2107846605999063322.md]

**Source omissions:** no exact foundation model/version, API endpoint, transcription tool, speech transport, integration/authentication/permissions design, import procedure/file format/path, storage/retrieval architecture, actual system-prompt text beyond the quoted questions, command, configuration, parameter, training recipe, reminder/scheduling mechanism, execution-review gate, eval dataset, code, or reproducible performance measurement is supplied. Connecting private accounts is advice, not demonstrated safe integration. No architecture is invented, no neighboring wiki stack is attributed to this author, and no source link or export menu was fetched or checked during this local-only ingest. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-how-to-stop-working-in-the-next-10-years-the-compl-2107846605999063322.md]
