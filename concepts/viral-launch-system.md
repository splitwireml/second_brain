## Overview

A methodology for creating viral product launches on X using a multi-agent Claude Code workflow. The system claims 95% of launch creation is automated, with 26 of 30 implementations going viral.

## Core Components

### BOLD CLAIM Framework
95% of viral launch success comes down to the "BOLD CLAIM" — what you are introducing and what makes it different from anything else. The claim must be:
- Novel (not already saturated in market)
- Solves a big pain point
- Immediately understandable

### 21-Agent System
Inside Claude Code, a group of 21 specialized agents each has one job:
- Market research agent
- Viral launch study agent
- Customer voice agent
- Novelty extraction agent
- Hook writing agent
- Hook critique agent
- Hook rewriting agent
- Narrative interest checker
- Product importance checker
- Manager agent for each worker

### Research-First Approach
Before writing anything, the system researches:
- Company, product, market, competitors
- Category and founder story
- Existing customer language around the problem
- Already-viral launches in similar categories

### Weapons Check
Every line evaluated on two dimensions:
1. **Invention novelty** — does it make product feel like something new?
2. **Copy intensity** — does it make someone feel something?

### Mom Test
An agent trained on a 61-year-old non-technical person to flag content that isn't mass-market accessible. Goal: content understandable by anyone, even non-technical or low IQ individuals.

## Key Principles

1. **Don't use Claude as a writer** — use it as an operating system
2. **Research before writing** — find strongest angle first
3. **Hook must answer 3 questions**: what is launched, why it matters, why never existed before
4. **Body makes claim feel real** — demo-driven narrative
5. **Aggressive filler cutting** — every line must justify existence
6. **Human edits final 5%** — taste and judgment still matter

## Operational Launch Sequence

A July 2026 X Article by [[fatimarizwan]] frames a viral launch as a staged operating sequence: product, one-page press release and FAQs, demo video, timing, pre-committed supporters and influencers, hook-first thread, launch-day amplification, newsletter, and follow-up. This extends the research, hook, and video layers already captured here with onboarding, coordination, and conversion steps. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-how-to-do-a-viral-launch-on-x-2079225170175398083.md]

The product gate is deliberately early: show value within 30 seconds and reach the product's "aha" within 60 seconds, then test the flow with five strangers before planning the launch. The press release becomes the source of truth for the video, thread, influencer packet, and newsletter; difficulty writing it clearly is treated as a product-clarity warning. These are source-described heuristics, not universal thresholds. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-how-to-do-a-viral-launch-on-x-2079225170175398083.md]

Distribution is scheduled rather than improvised. The article recommends recruiting aligned participants weeks ahead, using calendar reminders, coordinating early reposts and quote-tweets, publishing when the target audience is awake, and following up with engaged users after the first 48 hours. It also treats every feature, milestone, and integration as another launch, making repetition and audience compounding part of the system. Reach, influencer counts, timing, and conversion figures remain source-reported claims. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-how-to-do-a-viral-launch-on-x-2079225170175398083.md]

## Juan's four-stage X trending playbook

Juan's July 2026 X Article adds a complete launch-day operating sequence to this concept. It frames X distribution as staged sampling: a post is shown to a small audience, reactions in a short window determine whether it advances to larger circles, and a conversation that outgrows one post can become a platform-recognized subject. The article's operational variables are engagement speed, engagement composition, and conversation breadth. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-how-to-get-your-launch-trending-on-x-full-guide-2082138861136736272.md]

### Stage 1 — research

The source recommends building a Claude system from X data. It names twitterapi.io as a service that can provide a key in minutes at a fraction-of-a-cent-per-tweet cost without an approval process; those service and pricing claims are source-described. The study list should contain 50 or more accounts serving the target audience: direct competitors, adjacent products, and category companies launched in the previous six months. Open Claude Code, pull recent posts for each account, keep posts above that account's own average views, and inspect the first line, opening claim, video/image role, and requested reader action. The output is one view-ranked swipe file with a note explaining why each winning post worked. It is explicitly a pattern library, not a sentence-copying source. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-how-to-get-your-launch-trending-on-x-full-guide-2082138861136736272.md]

### Stage 2 — hook people

The first ten seconds are designed as one unit: a 60–90 second video, the first line, and a comment reason. The product's most impressive moment should be visible in the first five seconds and understandable with sound off; the source says not to lead with a logo or founder introduction. The first line is a claim, not a description: it can declare the old way finished, plant a first-of-its-kind flag, or attack a category practice the market already resents. The claim is pushed toward the edge of believable and tested on someone outside tech until it can be repeated over dinner. The first reply carries a free playbook, template, breakdown, or early access as a lead magnet in exchange for a comment; the source says this works close to 90% of the time and treats the resulting comments as both leads and high-cost signals. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-how-to-get-your-launch-trending-on-x-full-guide-2082138861136736272.md]

### Stage 3 — get attention

Creator coordination is divided by function: big accounts supply raw reach, mid-size niche experts supply buyer trust, and hundreds of small accounts supply breadth of voices. Briefs contain the exact posting minute, link, claim, and several angles so forty posts do not look like one press release; creators must keep their own voice. Big accounts and experts post in hour one, while small accounts roll out wave after wave for the next three hours. The source's target is 200 or more posts in the first four hours, intended to resemble discovery rather than a campaign. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-how-to-get-your-launch-trending-on-x-full-guide-2082138861136736272.md]

### Stage 4 — conversion

The conversion stage treats replies as fresh distribution: the founder answers every comment, DMs everyone who shared or quoted the launch on the same day, and delivers lead magnets quickly with a question about what the commenter is building. A five-person team is described as one person posting and four people in DMs. For 48 hours, the timeline is fed with plain-number milestones such as signups, views, and waitlist size; public replies to late large accounts; and new cuts from working material, including video clips, screenshots of unusual reactions, and a founder note. The source says to post nothing unrelated during that period and to keep showing up afterward because buyers may convert weeks later. These timings, counts, and performance claims are source-described rather than independently verified. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-how-to-get-your-launch-trending-on-x-full-guide-2082138861136736272.md]

The article gives no model/version, API endpoint, command, configuration, prompt, file format, or implementation detail for the Claude/Claude Code research system or twitterapi.io data handoff. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-how-to-get-your-launch-trending-on-x-full-guide-2082138861136736272.md]

## Startup influencer launches: launch recall and buyer memory

[[0xfjuan]]'s October 6, 2026 article distinguishes launch-day reach from being remembered when a buyer later needs the product. It reports **285K impressions with zero leads** for one post versus **55K impressions, four meetings, and one meeting turning into one of his biggest launches** for another. Reach alone would have favored the wrong post: the smaller audience included people who might actually buy. The source also reports **over $1M spent on X creators**. These are author-reported anecdotes/spending claims, not independent measurements or guaranteed launch outcomes. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-how-to-make-your-launch-unforgettable-2107458102710735032.md]

### 1. Choose who must remember the product and when

Define the future **buyer-memory use-case trigger**, not “everyone in tech”: a developer seeking a faster way to investigate failing tests, or a support lead answering the same customer question for the **fifth time that morning**. Inspect the launch from that buyer's perspective: do they recognize their problem in the **first few seconds**, and understand why the product is worth trying? Choose promotion surfaces accordingly; a smaller creator whose audience faces the problem may be more valuable than a large account followed by people who merely enjoy AI demos. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-how-to-make-your-launch-unforgettable-2107458102710735032.md]

### 2. Make recognition survive caption loss

Reposts, clips, and group-chat sharing can strip away the original caption. Evaluate the **video alone**: can viewers identify the product and what they can use it for? Put the product name where viewers will actually see it. Keep the product description/positioning consistent across the **founder's post, demo, creator posts, and landing page**. Give creators freedom to speak in their own voice while ensuring they accurately explain what the product does. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-how-to-make-your-launch-unforgettable-2107458102710735032.md]

Before launch, show the asset to someone who did not work on it; ask about it **the next day**. Remembering the result but not being able to name the company is a reason to revise the video. The source supplies no sample size, score, threshold, or formal recall-test instrumentation. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-how-to-make-your-launch-unforgettable-2107458102710735032.md]

**Wiki synthesis:** the caption-independent naming test can coexist with July's “do not lead with a logo” rule: visible product identification need not displace the opening payoff. The two sources address different checks, not a demonstrated causal relationship. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-how-to-get-your-launch-trending-on-x-full-guide-2082138861136736272.md] ^[raw/articles/xarticle-how-to-make-your-launch-unforgettable-2107458102710735032.md]

### 3. Use creators as recurring partners, not single-post purchases

Provide **early product access** so creators can find an audience-specific angle: a developer-facing account can show a real workflow; a founder-facing account can explain the problem its audience's team would solve. Making both accounts read the same script wastes the creator-specific value. After launch, read **replies** rather than only views: are target-market people asking how it works, describing a **use case**, **requesting access**, or discussing it with their team? ^[raw/articles/xarticle-how-to-make-your-launch-unforgettable-2107458102710735032.md]

For creators who start these conversations, plan a **second creator post within 1–2 weeks** (“the next week or two”). Give it something new: a different workflow, an answer to an audience question, or an example from a user **who has agreed to share it**. Continue working together if the relationship works; repeated returns to the product are proposed to strengthen the creator–product association, not proven to cause a specified recall uplift. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-how-to-make-your-launch-unforgettable-2107458102710735032.md]

### 4. Have a follow-up story and visible update mini-launches

Do not spend everything on the announcement and then repost the same trailer the following Monday. Plan the following week and the next few weeks from what users actually do: which question keeps recurring, what users made, what the team fixed, and which demo segment people want explained further. A short **screen recording** that answers a real question can provide a fresh reason to try the product without another expensive production. Treat **visible product updates as small launches (mini-launches)**, giving founder/team concrete recurring material and interested buyers a reason to look again. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-how-to-make-your-launch-unforgettable-2107458102710735032.md]

### 5. Observe returning buyers and feedback attribution

After the initial campaign, watch who mentions the product: **recommendation tags**, potential buyers returning with specific questions, and whether follow-up posts bring **useful conversations, demo requests, or signups**. Learn from **replies, DMs, sales calls**, and a simple **“Where did you hear about us?”** question. Perfect attribution is not required to distinguish a creator who attracts curious spectators from one who attracts people who want to use the product. The source specifies no attribution model, analytics product, tracking integration, measurement window beyond the stated follow-up periods, or numerical success cutoff. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-how-to-make-your-launch-unforgettable-2107458102710735032.md]

### Source boundary and commercial CTA

This is a buyer-fit/recall extension of the earlier launch-attention method, not another automated Claude/Claude Code/twitterapi.io pipeline; the October source names **X** and a promotional **Loom video**, but gives no AI model, command, prompt, API, configuration, or implementation stack. The article offers a personal Loom launch breakdown by DM; asks readers to DM **RECALL** for a **Launch Recall Checklist** covering checks before distribution spend and the **two weeks after launch**; and invites those launching in the **next 30 days** to describe what they are building and who it is for to receive a first-focus recommendation. The checklist/video are not included in the local export, and the offers are commercial framing rather than evidence that those services were delivered. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-how-to-make-your-launch-unforgettable-2107458102710735032.md]

## Launch Video Checklist

Mitchell's later launch-video article adds a craft checklist for the video asset itself, extending the earlier Claude Code launch system beyond research and copy. The working rule is that feed viewers are not captive: lead with the payoff, put the wildest product moment first, make the first five to seven seconds survive the retention cliff, and avoid jargon that signals "advertisement" before proof arrives. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-the-secret-to-making-any-launch-video-go-viral-2074916350280216904.md]

The execution layer is visual and iterative: accelerate the final cut by roughly 10%, keep static screenshots moving, sync voiceover and visuals tightly enough that a muted viewer can follow, prove the product on screen during the 5–30 second window, storyboard in Figma before animation, and expect around ten complete versions before launch. This connects the launch-system page to broader [[virality-mechanics]] and short-form video format research while staying specific to tech-product demos. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-the-secret-to-making-any-launch-video-go-viral-2074916350280216904.md]
