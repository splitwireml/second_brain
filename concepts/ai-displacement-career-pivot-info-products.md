# AI Displacement Career Pivot Info Products

**AI displacement career-pivot info products** are narrowly targeted guides, plans, and services for people whose work has been automated or who believe displacement is imminent. The source contrasts this urgent, outcome-driven market with generic "use AI to make money" and productivity courses aimed at people who are still employed and have time to browse.

## Market wedge

| Audience | Stated problem | Buying context |
|---|---|---|
| Aspirational AI learner | Wants to become more productive or make money eventually. | Low urgency; lower-ticket, delayed purchase. |
| Recently displaced worker | Needs a concrete next step before savings or unemployment support runs out. | High urgency; a shorter job search could plausibly justify a higher price. |
| Anxious employed worker | Wants to reduce the chance of being caught unprepared. | Prevention-oriented; broader audience but less immediate pain. |

The durable positioning move is to sell a **specific path to a specific next outcome**, not motivation or a generic tool catalog. The source's counts of competing courses, willingness-to-pay differences, and audience sizes are source claims, not market measurements.

## Product ladder

1. **30-day career-pivot plan** — assessment, transferable-skills audit, financial runway, skill-gap analysis, resume/LinkedIn repositioning, networking, interview preparation, and an emergency-income bridge.
2. **Job-category transition guides** — one guide per displaced role, such as customer service → customer success or data entry → data analysis. The promise is "this happened to me; show me the path for my category," not generic career advice.
3. **90-day displacement emergency kit** — immediate financial and administrative actions, rapid reskilling, repositioning, portfolio proof, job-search execution, negotiation, and first-90-days support.
4. **Prevention playbook** — warning signs, internal pivots, skills to develop, positioning, and side-income preparation for workers who are still employed.
5. **Services and institutional expansion** — guide → accountability coaching → hands-on job-search support → employer-funded or group outplacement. The source's price ladder is source-claimed and requires qualified delivery.

## Operating loop

1. Select one displaced job category and one plausible adjacent outcome.
2. Interview people who successfully made that transition; use career coaches or other subject-matter experts when the operator lacks expertise.
3. Turn the evidence into a time-bounded plan, worksheets, templates, and decision points. The source proposes roughly four weeks and 40–50 hours for an initial category guide; this is a source claim.
4. Publish free, immediately useful content: first-72-hour checklists, transferable-skill examples, role-transition maps, and interview-gap explanations.
5. Distribute where people are actively seeking help, including job-loss, career-change, and industry communities. Respect community rules and do not target individuals with manipulative fear-based outreach.
6. Use buyer questions, completion, outcomes, refunds, and qualified testimonials to improve the guide. Move only users who need accountability into higher-touch services.

## Ethical boundary

Urgency is a valid product signal, not permission to exploit vulnerability. A responsible offer should:

- make realistic, conditional promises rather than guarantee a salary or job date;
- distinguish general career education from legal, unemployment, financial, and mental-health advice;
- validate claims about "AI-resistant" roles against current occupational evidence instead of presenting them as permanent facts;
- include a clear scope, refund policy, support path, and escalation to qualified professionals;
- measure whether users make progress, not only whether they buy.

The source explicitly contrasts a specific, realistic transition plan with expensive generic freelancing promises. Its quoted refund rates, testimonial, ROI, and revenue projections are not independent evidence.
