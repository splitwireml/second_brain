# AI Influencer Marketing

Using AI-generated virtual creators (avatars) to promote apps and software products on social media, primarily Instagram and TikTok.

## Core Mechanics

**Why it works:** AI influencers are cheap ($39 for an avatar), fast to produce, don't demand payment or equity, and can be scaled to 5+ accounts simultaneously. The content format — usually short-form video — drives organic reach when volume is applied.

**The 3-box framework** ([[ernesto-lopez]]):
1. Pick a painkiller app (solves a specific, felt problem)
2. Build killer onboarding (converts the traffic once it arrives)
3. Add a good CTA (explicit call to action in every video)

**Algorithm strategy:** Post 10+ reels per day. Most will underperform; 1 in 10 or 1 in 100 goes viral and drives outsized downloads. Volume + speed + research on what's working = the formula.

**CTAs that work:** Direct download links, "start free trial" moments woven into the video narrative.

## Recurring DTC creative retainer and owned ad spots (Machina, 2026-10-06)

Separate from the earlier app-install/volume strategy, Machina's Higgsfield-sponsored source sells a **refill schedule**: monthly new creatives, one recognizable character, and proven formats from ONE chosen product niche. Its creative-fatigue rationale is attributed to Meta by the source, not independently researched here. Select a **real DTC brand already running ads**, then make a few spec ads with the AI influencer **holding the brand's product** in a winning format. The proposed range is **$1,500 to $10,000 per month depending on volume**. The exact pitch is: ^[raw/articles/xarticle-how-to-get-rich-in-the-attention-economy-ai-influe-2107476083851534377.md]

> "here are a few ads i made for you with my AI influencer, i can send you a steady stream of these every month - $1,500 to $10,000 a month depending on how many you need"

The article calls **every 2 to 4 weeks on Meta and weekly on TikTok** refresh benchmarks "floating around" rather than presenting a measured ad-lifetime benchmark. Once an account grows, it proposes **selling ad spots on the influencer's own account to brands**. Its scale plan is the same character across TikTok/Instagram/more, more creatives per brand, multiple niche-specific accounts, and multiple influencers with **separate identity sheets and Soul ID**. These are source-described business moves, not sales/results verified in this ingest. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-how-to-get-rich-in-the-attention-economy-ai-influe-2107476083851534377.md]

The source's disclosure constraints are preserved as **attributed, unverified current rules**: TikTok **AI-generated label for realistic AI content**; ads with **undisclosed AI content rejected or restricted**; branded TikTok posts' **commercial content disclosure toggle**; Meta **paid partnership tagging with the business partner**; and FTC **brand-connection disclosure in the post itself**, with an **own clear disclosure line** because the platform tool alone should not be assumed sufficient. The author says "label everything." This local capture did not research current laws or platform rules. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-how-to-get-rich-in-the-attention-economy-ai-influe-2107476083851534377.md]
