# Research Skill Building

A framework for becoming better at research by treating originality, judgment, and speed of learning as trainable habits rather than innate traits. The source essay argues that good research emerges from better problem selection, better inputs, explicit written thinking, and faster feedback loops.

## Core Claims

### Pick problems instead of inheriting them
- Do not default to whatever the field is currently rewarding socially
- Work backward from outcomes you genuinely want to exist
- Predict results before experiments so taste becomes trainable rather than mystical

### Upgrade the input stream
- Read primary sources rather than summaries of summaries
- Mine older foundational material because many "new" directions are rediscoveries
- Build enough range across systems, statistics, evals, and adjacent disciplines to detect weak work early

### Write to expose your own mistakes
- Keep explicit logs of hypothesis, setup, expectation, result, and updated belief
- Publish clear explanations because public writing both sharpens thought and compounds reputation
- Treat writing as an anti-self-deception tool rather than just communication polish

### Tighten experimental loops
- Optimize for how quickly you discover that you are wrong
- Shrink experiments until they are cheap and debuggable
- Treat tooling, reproducibility, and baseline discipline as research accelerators rather than side work

### Inspect outputs directly
- Read failures, transcripts, and strange edge cases instead of staring only at aggregate metrics
- Spend real time with raw data because many ML failures are silent data problems

### Wander and network on purpose
- Explore multiple subfields before committing too early
- Let weak ideas die cheaply
- Keep an open door for conversation, replication, and public feedback because collaboration compounds information flow

## Practical Value

This is a strong operating model for AI/ML researchers and serious builders because it reframes research as an evidence loop. It aligns with [[research-agent-vault]] on explicit receipts and with [[andrej-karpathy]] on shrinking experiments until they are inspectable and cheap.
