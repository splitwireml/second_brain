# Meta Ad Funnel Architectures

A funnel is an architecture for moving a stranger from an ad to a purchase or booked call: the page sequence, qualification steps, belief shift, proof, and conversion mechanism all matter. Zack's source describes twelve reusable Meta-ad funnel types and argues that the right choice depends on offer price, audience awareness, and how much trust or education is required before the CTA. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-every-winning-meta-ad-funnel-explained-in-1-articl-2078498450430382470.md]

## The twelve architectures

1. **Call funnel** — ad → application landing page → qualification → calendar → confirmation and reminders. Best suited, in the source's framework, to high-ticket services that require a sales conversation; an extra opt-in usually adds friction without enough benefit.
2. **Advertorial funnel** — ad → editorial-style article → product or offer page → thank-you page. The article educates, shifts the reader's beliefs, introduces the mechanism, and delays the product recommendation until the reader understands the problem and solution.
3. **VSL funnel** — ad → video sales letter → delayed CTA → order or booking page. The video carries the argument, proof, objections, and offer when the prospect needs substantial education and trust-building.
4. **Quiz funnel** — ad → five-to-seven-question quiz → segmented results page → CTA. Conditional answers create a personalized recommendation; capture belongs at the results stage after the prospect has invested attention.
5. **Lead-magnet funnel** — ad → opt-in → resource delivery → email nurture → main offer. A genuinely useful guide, checklist, template, or calculator qualifies interest while the sequence converts prospects who need several touchpoints.
6. **Webinar funnel** — ad → registration → reminders → live or evergreen webinar → application or booking. Teaching carries most of the belief shift, with the offer presented after the audience has received context and proof.
7. **Comparison-page funnel** — ad → honest comparison → booking or purchase CTA. This fits solution-aware prospects already weighing DIY, in-house, freelancers, agencies, or competing products.
8. **Listicle funnel** — ad → scannable list article → CTA in the final item. It uses the advertorial logic in a format designed for readers who skim rather than commit to a long narrative.
9. **DM funnel** — ad → keyword DM → automated opener → conversational qualification → booked call. The conversation can build trust and qualify more naturally than a form when the audience is active on social and a setter can preserve the founder's voice.
10. **Telegram/community funnel** — ad → free community → daily value → periodic offers. This replaces immediate conversion pressure with repeated exposure, social proof, and nurture for long consideration cycles.
11. **Challenge funnel** — ad → challenge registration → three-to-five days of content → final-day offer. Daily participation escalates commitment and can demonstrate a result before the paid continuation is offered.
12. **Straight product-page funnel** — ad → product or service page → checkout or booking. It is simplest when the audience is product-aware or most-aware, but the source positions it as weak for cold traffic because there is no pre-selling layer.

## Selection rules

The source's practical heuristic is to match the funnel to the economic and psychological job:

- A $3,000–$10,000+ service with a sales close starts with the call funnel; add advertorial education later if cold traffic needs more belief-shifting.
- A $500–$2,000 offer without a sales call fits an advertorial or VSL path when the mechanism must be understood before purchase.
- A $100–$500 offer can use quiz or lead-magnet segmentation and nurture when personalization or repeated exposure improves the decision.
- Product-aware or retargeted traffic can use the direct product page; cold traffic usually needs an intermediate education, proof, or qualification layer.

The broader rule is to optimize one matched architecture before stacking complexity. Skipping a needless opt-in can protect conversion, while a carefully placed advertorial, quiz, webinar, or nurture sequence earns its friction by solving an awareness or trust problem. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-every-winning-meta-ad-funnel-explained-in-1-articl-2078498450430382470.md]

## Evidence status

The source reports the author's experience across service-business Meta ad spend and gives conversion-rate, price-point, spend-cap, and outcome comparisons. Those figures are source-claimed and are not independently audited here. The architectures are best treated as hypotheses to test against offer economics, audience awareness, funnel-step conversion, lead quality, show rate, close rate, and downstream revenue.
