# Pounds

Pounds (`@pounddz`) is an X Article author documenting a source-described Instagram affiliate system for peptide offers. The article claims a non-viral path to consistent `$500-$1,000/day`, but the outcome, conversion, and platform claims are source-described and unverified. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-how-i-built-a-500day-affiliate-system-on-instagram-2085058455597990043.md]

## Source-described Instagram affiliate system (2026-08-05)

The source's content layer uses five Instagram accounts with different AI avatars. Each video is framed as a mini VSL for a narrowly defined problem: intensify the pain, present a unique mechanism, and route attention to a free guide. The proposed funnel uses a Manychat comment trigger: a viewer comments a specific word, receives the guide, and enters a name and email address to access it. The source recommends top-of-funnel, middle-of-funnel, and bottom-of-funnel content rather than only problem-awareness posts. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-how-i-built-a-500day-affiliate-system-on-instagram-2085058455597990043.md]

Pounds names the **R.A.C.E. method**: **Relate** by describing a specific group's problem and struggles better than the audience can; **Advise** by giving knowledge and unique mechanisms for solving it; **Captivate** by routing viewers into an email list instead of wasting a one-time view; and **Enroll** by using the built distribution asset to drive the sale, lead, download, or other end goal. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-how-i-built-a-500day-affiliate-system-on-instagram-2085058455597990043.md]

The source's creative reference is the “purple cow authority method”: an extraordinary character such as a monk stops the scroll and supplies an authority reason to listen, here in a natural-health-remedy context. It contrasts visibly AI-looking Facebook-page content that routes to Amazon affiliate products with content intended to be unidentifiable as AI and suitable for Gen Z. The source describes a Telegram video tutorial invitation but does not provide implementation details for that tutorial. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-how-i-built-a-500day-affiliate-system-on-instagram-2085058455597990043.md]

The owned-audience claim is that only `3%` of an audience is ready to buy immediately, leaving `97%` for nurture; after the lead is captured, targeted email campaigns are run with Claude, including the source-described possibility of sending one email every day for six months. No email provider, sending configuration, deliverability control, or measurement method is specified. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-how-i-built-a-500day-affiliate-system-on-instagram-2085058455597990043.md]

## Offer and conversion mechanics

Pounds says Amazon brands require millions of views to become worthwhile, while peptide offers can convert on doctor appointments being set rather than on a sale. The source presents that appointment event as lower-friction and “almost a CPA offer.” It names Whoosh on Glitchy as an example and promotes signing up through Glitchy for approval; the offer terms, approval process, appointment qualification, payout, and tracking implementation are not supplied. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-how-i-built-a-500day-affiliate-system-on-instagram-2085058455597990043.md]

The video is not supposed to close the sale. Its job is to create enough curiosity and belief that the viewer wants the free guide and comments; the emails can become more specific and aggressive and are where the source says the sale occurs. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-how-i-built-a-500day-affiliate-system-on-instagram-2085058455597990043.md]

## Research and scripting workflow

1. Search Reddit for a specific painful problem, such as losing weight for women going through menopause, and copy threads and comments into a messy Google Doc.
2. Feed that raw-data document to Claude and request a research brief containing audience avatars, top product objections, biggest struggles, and the way people describe the problem.
3. Give Claude the source's hook prompt: “give me 25 different 8-10 second hooks based on the document I gave you for UGC using captivating initial psycological loops,” followed by the examples “This may surprise you...”, “I just figured this out...”, “I wish I knew this earlier...”, “Pause for a second...”, “Here's the real truth...”, and “Ever notice this pattern?”.
4. Ask Claude which `10` hooks it believes will retain customer attention, then request a `40 second` body for those hooks.

The source says the resulting scripts are a starting point: the operator must change them, while Claude is useful for angles and hooks. It then routes the scripts into the email campaign rather than treating the short video as the sale. The local source names Claude but gives no model/version, API, command, configuration, prompt interface, email software, file path, or execution handoff beyond the Google Doc and copy/paste steps. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-how-i-built-a-500day-affiliate-system-on-instagram-2085058455597990043.md]

## Pixar-animation ad and affiliate branches (2026-09-27)

Pounds (`@pounddz`) describes a source-claimed `$20k/month` business around AI animations he says can look “straight out of the Pixar office.” He says interest began after seeing Franky Shaw post them, then reports hours of tests, `100s of dollars` spent, and many failures; the income, quality, attention, virality, paid-ad prevalence, and “gold rush of the next 2 months” framing remain source-described rather than independently verified. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-how-i-make-20kmonth-with-pixar-animations-and-how--2104262810473787872.md]

His first stated revenue path is freelance ad creation / creative strategy: brands reportedly pay `$300-$500 per ad`; he says he has `2 clients` plus others, and takes `3-5%` of ad spend for videos that run. The second is consulting on `SAAS + Creative teams`, teaching ideas and the animation process. He explicitly says he does not use AI to originate ideas, instead citing walks in nature, and says a paid info product is only an interest because of demand, not a launched offer. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-how-i-make-20kmonth-with-pixar-animations-and-how--2104262810473787872.md]

The article separately labels a `$100K/Month Affiliate Play`: an acquaintance allegedly runs `CPI offers` from Glitchy such as Freecash with a new attention-retaining animation format. It also labels a `$50k/Month Organic Affiliate Play`: another person reportedly makes organic brand content for `$2k/month per page + an RPM + affiliate commissions`, using prior Facebook and Instagram page connections. The body does not supply the first acquaintance's numeric amount, offer contract, payout/tracking implementation, prompts, renderer, platform interface, or independent outcomes. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-how-i-make-20kmonth-with-pixar-animations-and-how--2104262810473787872.md]

The source's learn-and-practice sequence names `D.R. Copywriting`, `Marketing Psychology`, `Omni`, `Seedance`, `Nano Banana Pro`, `Claude`, `Basic Directing`, and social-media algorithms; it recommends *Breakthrough Advertising*, intentional teardown of viral videos, and a source-described `20 hours` / roughly one-week learning estimate. It then says to choose Instagram, TikTok, or Youtube, go all in, make animations for affiliate offers via Glitchy, frame content around a known ICP (or learn how that ICP talks, feels, and behaves), and post outputs on X. These are source-described recommendations; no model/version, command, parameter, prompt, API, file format/path, or cross-tool handoff is given, and no adjacent workflow is inferred. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-how-i-make-20kmonth-with-pixar-animations-and-how--2104262810473787872.md]
