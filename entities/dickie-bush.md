# Dickie Bush

X/Twitter creator (@dickiebush) known for writing, digital product monetization, and Ship 30 for 30.

## Background

Former Wall Street analyst who began posting on X in mid-2020, writing daily threads curating takeaways from podcasts. After 27 days of near-zero traction, his 28th thread went viral after being retweeted by Naval Ravikant.

## Digital Products

### Podcast Compendium ($400)

First digital product — a Notion document with podcast summaries sold on Gumroad for $29. Taught three lessons: no large audience needed to launch, first product should be work already done, people pay for organized thinking.

### Ship 30 for 30 ($2,000,000+)

Daily writing accountability cohort co-founded with Nicolas Cole. First cohort launched with 50+ paying members. Grew to 7 figures in revenue within 12 months. The product targets "beginner writers starting to write online" — a specific niche-within-niche (Mega-Category: Writing → Niche: Internet/Digital Writing → Niche-Within-Niche: Beginners).

## Claims

- Over $20,000,000 generated from digital products over 6 years (source claim, unverified)
- $2,000,000+ from Ship 30 for 30 alone (source claim)
- First cohort of Ship 30 hit 7 figures within 12 months (source claim)

## Multi-platform content system

Dickie Bush's 2026-07-20 article presents a source-described content engine built around one CTA per platform, a fixed cadence filled deliberately rather than inspiration-led posting, and gradual expansion from one weekly newsletter plus one short-form post after 90 days of consistency. The system assigns different roles to X, LinkedIn, Instagram, Substack Notes, Threads, and YouTube, then turns roughly 20 net-new pieces into 160 weekly posts through production and reposting. These cadence and algorithm observations are source-reported, not independently verified. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-how-to-maximize-your-content-output-across-all-soc-2079228257401184282.md]

The article is a concrete creator-business example of [[content-strategy]] and complements [[content-os]] with a human/team production model rather than an AI-only workflow.

## Astra operating model (2026-09-24)

Dickie Bush's local X Article says he used **GPT-6 Astra** exclusively inside the Codex/ChatGPT desktop harness for ten days on a $200/month plan and now treats it as his default AI surface for multi-tool work. He describes its browser/computer operation, vague-instruction planning, and long sequential-task tracking as the reasons to move his own role toward deciding requirements and reviewing builds rather than manually carrying out routine execution. These adoption, capability, plan-price, and reliability statements are source-described, not independently verified. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-gpt-6-astra-has-completely-changed-the-way-i-run-m-2102753461796262232.md]

For Vortex Mastermind, the source reports a background build of a landing page, embedded Typeform, lightweight Airtable CRM, Slack application alert, GitHub/Vercel hosting, custom domain, Calendly booking, email automations, and Zapier connections. Its proposed operating loop is first-pass desired outcome → tool access → V1 review and new decisions → plain-English iterations → end-to-end test plus bullet summary. The source also offers a Deciding & Doing project-breakdown prompt and promotes a Ship 30 for 30 cohort; neither offer, rollout, nor result is independently evaluated here.
