## Six-step first-draft workflow

Cole's 2026-07-26 local X Article describes a source-specific way to turn a blank page into a finished draft: remove perfection pressure, write a deliberately rough first pass, and assemble the piece iteratively rather than trying to write from the first word to the last in one sitting. He compares the draft to Michelangelo's block of marble that was gradually shaped into “David”; the writer's first job is to pour disjointed or raw thoughts onto the page. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-how-to-turn-a-blank-page-into-a-finished-draft-in--2081358806408106390.md]

The article separates five drafts: Draft 1 asks “What am I trying to say to WHO? Why? And what are the big ideas?”; Draft 2 adds what is missing; Draft 3 removes what does not need to be said; Draft 4 finds faster ways to say it; and Draft 5 refines, clarifies, and makes the language more specific. Draft 1 is an outline of the main points, built by “freight train” bulleting without sentences, spelling, grammar, or backspacing until there is nothing left to say and the itch has stopped. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-how-to-turn-a-blank-page-into-a-finished-draft-in--2081358806408106390.md]

The six operating steps are: define a reader/problem/benefit “North Star” with Cole's Pinpoint Writing questions; run a 10-minute bullet-point brain dump and group it into an outline; brainstorm five more increasingly specific headline ideas; prep a blank page with the North Star or revised headline, subheads, and ordered main points; fill each outline section with useful details, steps, examples, reasons, mistakes, and stories; then marinate the draft for a day or two, reread it with fresh eyes, edit, and publish before it feels completely ready. The reusable method is filed as [[first-draft-writing-workflow]]. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-how-to-turn-a-blank-page-into-a-finished-draft-in--2081358806408106390.md]

The article also uses partner [[dickie-bush]] as a concrete example: outline ideas on a morning walk, fill the draft from 6 to 7:30 AM, read it aloud and edit the next morning, then publish. The source ends by promoting a free “Start Writing Online” crash course and a 13,000-word Ultimate Guide + email course; those promotional details are source-described, not independently verified. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-how-to-turn-a-blank-page-into-a-finished-draft-in--2081358806408106390.md]

## Writing as career evidence

Cole's 2026-08-03 X Article argues that people who are not trying to become influencers, start a side hustle, or build a business should still publish consistently because public writing can become career evidence. Its contrast is a source-described thought experiment: a candidate with a master-crafted resume loses a dream role and a large sign-on bonus to an otherwise identical candidate who has written online for years. Cole says digital-first employers want to see the person already doing the work, current insights rather than only what they did three years ago, and public experience rather than fluffed-up resume claims. These are source claims, not independently verified hiring findings. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-creating-content-is-gasoline-for-your-career-its-t-2084253831127576705.md]

The article gives five source-described personal-growth effects of writing every day: a publishing streak builds accountability that can spill into nutrition, exercise, and important relationships; writing creates emotional as well as thought clarity by revealing what matters and reinforcing a worldview (Cole calls this listening for internal “signal”); writing volume actively creates a voice, with conscious experiments and blended archetypes; publishing builds self-confidence through practicing ideas and convictions, articulating them better, and learning to trust oneself; and the time required to stack thoughts into a perspective teaches patience with oneself, one's thoughts, writing, and other people. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-creating-content-is-gasoline-for-your-career-its-t-2084253831127576705.md]

Cole frames the opportunity as arbitrage: most people read, lurk, and listen without publishing, so he source-claims that simply posting can place someone among the top 1% in a field, scale a personal brand, attract opportunities, and open elite networks. The source provides no platform-selection method, publishing cadence beyond “consistently”/“every day,” measurement design, hiring sample, or independent evidence for the top-1% and opportunity outcomes. It embeds a separate deep dive on a consistent writing voice with AI at `https://x.com/i/status/2080270972603826577`; only that link is available in this local article, so its contents were not inferred. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-creating-content-is-gasoline-for-your-career-its-t-2084253831127576705.md]

These are promotional, source-described claims; the guide, crash course, email course, audience result, and linked destination were not independently inspected. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-creating-content-is-gasoline-for-your-career-its-t-2084253831127576705.md]
