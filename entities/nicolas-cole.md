## Six-step first-draft workflow

Cole's 2026-07-26 local X Article describes a source-specific way to turn a blank page into a finished draft: remove perfection pressure, write a deliberately rough first pass, and assemble the piece iteratively rather than trying to write from the first word to the last in one sitting. He compares the draft to Michelangelo's block of marble that was gradually shaped into “David”; the writer's first job is to pour disjointed or raw thoughts onto the page. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-how-to-turn-a-blank-page-into-a-finished-draft-in--2081358806408106390.md]

The article separates five drafts: Draft 1 asks “What am I trying to say to WHO? Why? And what are the big ideas?”; Draft 2 adds what is missing; Draft 3 removes what does not need to be said; Draft 4 finds faster ways to say it; and Draft 5 refines, clarifies, and makes the language more specific. Draft 1 is an outline of the main points, built by “freight train” bulleting without sentences, spelling, grammar, or backspacing until there is nothing left to say and the itch has stopped. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-how-to-turn-a-blank-page-into-a-finished-draft-in--2081358806408106390.md]

The six operating steps are: define a reader/problem/benefit “North Star” with Cole's Pinpoint Writing questions; run a 10-minute bullet-point brain dump and group it into an outline; brainstorm five more increasingly specific headline ideas; prep a blank page with the North Star or revised headline, subheads, and ordered main points; fill each outline section with useful details, steps, examples, reasons, mistakes, and stories; then marinate the draft for a day or two, reread it with fresh eyes, edit, and publish before it feels completely ready. The reusable method is filed as [[first-draft-writing-workflow]]. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-how-to-turn-a-blank-page-into-a-finished-draft-in--2081358806408106390.md]

The article also uses partner [[dickie-bush]] as a concrete example: outline ideas on a morning walk, fill the draft from 6 to 7:30 AM, read it aloud and edit the next morning, then publish.
