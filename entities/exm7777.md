# Machina (@exm7777)

## Overview

Machina is the operator interviewed in VETTED's article on using AI to grow an existing business. The source presents him as an agency builder and AI-systems operator focused on leverage, research, and gradually removing himself from repeatable work rather than selling AI hype.^[raw/articles/xarticle-how-creators-can-actually-grow-their-business-with-2077109780498227601.md]

## Source-described operating thesis

- AI is leverage behind a business, not the business itself.
- Start with one department for one niche of solopreneurs or small businesses, then reuse a simple delivery skeleton across clients.
- Build a research system first: monitor the niche, competitors, and sources; produce a morning brief; feed content, offers, and outreach from that evidence.
- Earn autonomy through review: a task moves to the system only after repeated iterations produce no required changes.^[raw/articles/xarticle-how-creators-can-actually-grow-their-business-with-2077109780498227601.md]

The article also attributes to Machina a preference for practitioner-led inputs, human control over final content, and concentrated experiments in one niche and business model before rotating. These are source-reported views, not independently verified business results.^[raw/articles/xarticle-how-creators-can-actually-grow-their-business-with-2077109780498227601.md]

## Source-described five-lane operating blueprint

Machina's July 25 X Article describes a one-person business built from two assets: proven software that already handles jobs such as Stripe billing, CRM pipeline, booking-tool scheduling, and Meta ad delivery; and the operator's business knowledge about clients, pricing, qualified leads, escalation, and service delivery. The missing operator between those assets is supplied by an AI worker. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-how-to-build-and-scale-a-one-person-business-with--2081017272924361162.md]

The fastest path is [[viktor]], which the source describes as joining Slack or Microsoft Teams, using managed connectors for the CRM and ad platform among thousands of tools, and running scheduled jobs from a sentence in chat. The setup is one channel per lane—content, projects, outreach, finance, and ads—with pinned context, an invited Viktor, and no sending tool in outreach. Its exact lane briefs include X research over the last 14 days yielding 5 posts plus 1 thread, 8:00 under-8-line standups, research on 10 businesses with top-5 first-touch drafts and no sending, draft-only invoice creation plus Friday 16:00 finance summaries, and paused ad campaigns requiring explicit go. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-how-to-build-and-scale-a-one-person-business-with--2081017272924361162.md]

The same source describes a custom alternative: one agent, one job; separate research, writer, and reviewer roles; and four parts per specialist—durable identity file, only the needed knowledge-base slice, bounded job memory, and a schedule/gate. Identity and memory stay separate so learned churn cannot rewrite the agent's scope. The business brain is an Obsidian markdown vault organized into offer, client, voice, playbooks, and rulings; memory levels are files, a store such as mem0, then a graph, with few-hundred-line always-loaded caps, review dates, replaced facts, and a source-reported 10,000-to-about-200 retention example. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-how-to-build-and-scale-a-one-person-business-with--2081017272924361162.md]

The operating controls are a context-carrying brief, blast-radius permissions, two daily review windows, a hard monthly spend cap, quote-before-execute, and a scaling gate that adds the next lane only when the previous lane runs without a prompt. The article claims $100 in free Viktor credits covers the five-lane setup without a card; that claim and the product capabilities remain source-described. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-how-to-build-and-scale-a-one-person-business-with--2081017272924361162.md]
