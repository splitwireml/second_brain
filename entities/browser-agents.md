# browser-agents

Concept describing AI agents that operate within or control web browsers for autonomous web interaction. Related to [[autobrowse-browser-agent-memory]] and [[browserbase-autobrowse-agent-memory]] patterns.

## Business-context → browser evidence → SEO artifacts

Sarvesh Shrivastava's August 19 “Grok Bot” article is a concrete source-described browser-agent use case: load business NAP/services/areas/keywords/competitors/prior work and output preferences once; open Chrome to inspect Maps/GBP or logged-in SEMrush/Ahrefs/GSC/GA4 and research Wikidata/Rich Results; produce comparison spreadsheets, missing-field/action lists, ready-to-use copy/calendars, outreach and monthly performance reports. The article says “Grok Bot does the gathering. you do the thinking.” It specifies prompts and expected handoffs, not an executed integration, a Grok model/API or a proof of product capabilities. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-top-20-grok-bot-prompts-for-seo-the-only-stack-you-2090071557590900974.md]

The mature [[claude-cowork-seo-system]] preserves context and schedule without transferring Cowork's extension/files into Grok. [[browser-agent-local-seo-gbp]], [[browser-agent-local-seo-website]], [[browser-agent-local-seo-authority]] and [[browser-agent-local-seo-content-tracking]] preserve all 20 literal prompts. This pattern separates the browser surface and first-party/tool evidence from the language-model brand; requested data visibility and SEO outcomes remain unverified source claims. ^[raw/articles/xarticle-top-20-grok-bot-prompts-for-seo-the-only-stack-you-2090071557590900974.md]
