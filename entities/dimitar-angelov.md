# Dimitar Angelov

X creator (@dimitarangg) focused on B2B lead generation, deliverability-aware [[cold-email]] systems, and occasional [[link-post]] bookmarks.

## Overview

Makes the case that Apollo.io leads are oversaturated (250M contacts hammered by thousands of agencies), and that the alternative is scraping untapped databases — LinkedIn job-changers, Twitter discussions of pain points, Reddit problem-seekers, Crunchbase-funded startups, Product Hunt makers, and more.

Argues that [[claude]] Opus 4.7's visual accuracy upgrade enables scraping platforms that were previously too difficult.

The later 2026-06-27 bookmark captured here is an export-failed [[link-post]] containing only a shortened URL, so it extends provenance for the account without adding recoverable new claims.^[raw/articles/xarticle-httpstcoza1acztalq-2070896784449171568.md]

A June 2026 X article adds a much fuller operating picture: dedicated sending domains instead of the main brand domain, SPF/DKIM/DMARC hygiene, 30-day inbox warmup, low-volume daily caps, buying-signal-driven list building, bait-and-switch front-end offers, and sub-minute reply handling as the highest-leverage conversion variable.^[raw/articles/xarticle-the-most-valuable-cold-outreach-masterclass-ever-c-2071980383520895262.md]

## Key Claims

- Apollo leads have 2% reply rates because they've been pitched 50+ times
- Solution: scrape platforms where ICP actually congregates but competitors haven't reached
- 15+ untapped database categories listed (LinkedIn, Twitter, Reddit, Crunchbase, Product Hunt, Discord/Slack, Google Maps, universities, events, etc.)
- Offer: scraped 10,000+ untapped B2B leads via done-for-you service
- Cold outreach should be run as infrastructure plus diagnostics: separate sending domains, warmup, reputation checks, and failure-mode analysis before blaming copy
- Intent-sourced leads from public buying signals outperform broad scraped lists on reply rate, according to the author
- Positive-reply handling speed is a major booking bottleneck; the target is under 60 seconds during business hours
