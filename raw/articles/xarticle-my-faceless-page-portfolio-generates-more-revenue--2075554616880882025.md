# my faceless page portfolio generates more  revenue than the average McDonald's franchise profit

my faceless page portfolio generates more  revenue than the average McDonald's franchise profit

$492,000 a year from anonymous pages.

$250,000 a year from a McDonald's.

the McDonald's requires a $1,300,000 investment, 50 plus employees, a physical location, 18-hour operating days, and health inspections.

my portfolio requires $25 a month in tools, 0 employees, a couch, and checking my phone while brushing my teeth.

let me show you this comparison in full because i've been sitting with these numbers for 6 months and the more i look at them the more uncomfortable they make me in a way i think is worth sharing.

not uncomfortable because i feel guilty.

uncomfortable because the comparison reveals something about how we've been taught to think about what a real business looks like that i'm not sure we've fully processed yet.

here's the McDonald's math first because i want to be precise about it.

the average McDonald's franchise generates $3,200,000 in annual gross revenue.

that number sounds extraordinary until you see what's underneath it.

food cost: approximately $960,000. 30% of revenue.

labor: approximately $800,000. 25% of revenue.

rent and occupancy: approximately $480,000. 15% of revenue.

royalties to McDonald's corporate: approximately $160,000. 5% of revenue.

marketing fees to McDonald's corporate: approximately $128,000. 4% of revenue.

equipment maintenance and repairs: approximately $96,000. 3% of revenue.

utilities: approximately $64,000. 2% of revenue.

insurance: approximately $48,000. 1.5% of revenue.

miscellaneous operating costs: approximately $214,000. 6.7% of revenue.

total costs: approximately $2,950,000.

net profit: approximately $250,000.

on $3,200,000 in revenue.

a 7.8% margin.

after a $1,300,000 initial investment that took most franchisees 7-10 years to save or finance.

$250,000 a year.

for the person who invested $1,300,000, manages 50 employees, operates a physical location for 18 hours a day, passes health inspections, navigates corporate franchise requirements, handles food safety liability, manages labor turnover that averages 130% annually in fast food, and is personally responsible for a location that has a fixed address, a fixed lease, and a fixed identity that cannot be moved or sold easily if the neighborhood changes.

now here's my portfolio math.

annual gross revenue: $492,000.

tool costs: claude $20, canva $55 team plan, scheduling tool $89. $164 a month. $1,968 a year.

VA costs: 4 VAs managing 14 pages. $5,840 a month. $70,080 a year.

gumroad fees: approximately 3% of revenue. $14,760 a year.

total costs: $86,808 a year.

net revenue: $405,192 a year.

margin: 82.4%.

on an initial investment of approximately $2,400 spread across the first 6 months of building the first page.

not $1,300,000.

$2,400.

the McDonald's operator invested $1,300,000 and generates $250,000 in annual profit.

that's a 19.2% annual return on invested capital.

which is actually a good return by traditional business standards.

i invested $2,400 and generate $405,192 in annual profit.

that's a 16,883% annual return on invested capital.

i am not saying this to be provocative.

i am saying it because i spent 6 months checking and rechecking these numbers because i couldn't believe they were real and they are real and i think the implications are worth looking at directly.

now let me show you the full operational comparison because revenue and profit are only part of the picture.

THE INVESTMENT COMPARISON

McDonald's: $1,300,000 upfront. broken down as $500,000 liquid capital required by corporate, $500,000-$800,000 equipment and buildout, franchise fee of $45,000.

most franchisees finance a significant portion. the debt service on a $700,000 loan at 6.5% over 10 years is $7,941 a month. $95,292 a year. before the business generates a dollar.

my portfolio: $2,400 across 6 months. $20 claude subscription. $55 canva. $89 scheduling tool. $420 for the first VA. build costs for the first product which were absorbed by tools already being paid.

no loan. no debt service. no $1,300,000 that had to exist before the business could exist.

the McDonald's operator spent 7-10 years accumulating the capital to start.

i spent one tuesday afternoon.

THE EMPLOYEE COMPARISON

McDonald's: 50 plus employees. hiring, training, scheduling, managing, firing, rehiring, retraining, rescheduling. 130% annual turnover in fast food means the average location replaces its entire staff every 9 months. 50 employees times 130% turnover means 65 new hires a year. 65 interviews. 65 onboarding processes. 65 training periods. 65 chances for something to go wrong during the transition.

payroll processing. tax withholding. workers compensation. unemployment insurance. sexual harassment policies. break schedules. overtime calculations. shift swaps. call-outs at 6am on a saturday.

my portfolio: 4 VAs on contract. no payroll. no withholding. no workers compensation. no unemployment insurance. no break schedules. no overtime. no call-outs at 6am. contracts not employment. if a VA underperforms i post on workana and have 30 applications within 48 hours.

jessa has been with me 18 months.

maria 10 months.

daniel 7 months.

priya 6 weeks.

zero turnover in the portfolio's history.

because the job is clear, the documentation is complete, the expectations are explicit, and the work can be done from anywhere with a laptop and a wifi connection.

which means i attract people who value flexibility and keep them because the flexibility is the compensation.

THE LOCATION COMPARISON

McDonald's: a physical building. a specific address. a lease that typically runs 20 years because McDonald's corporate owns most of the real estate and leases it back to franchisees at above-market rates. a building that requires maintenance. equipment that breaks. a parking lot that needs repaving. a roof that eventually needs replacing. a grease trap that needs servicing. a dining room that gets vandalized occasionally. a drive-through speaker that stops working at 7am on a tuesday.

fixed. immovable. expensive to exit.

my portfolio: a couch.

specifically the same couch i bought 3 years ago from a furniture store that no longer exists for $600.

the couch has not required maintenance.

the couch has not needed its grease trap serviced.

the couch has not been vandalized.

the couch works exactly as well in a different city as it works in this one.

if i decide to spend 3 months in lisbon the couch stays here and the portfolio comes with me.

if i decide the city i'm in is no longer where i want to be the portfolio moves in the time it takes to update my gumroad payout account to a new bank.

the McDonald's does not move.

the McDonald's is the opposite of mobile.

the McDonald's is a 20-year commitment to a specific address in a specific city that may or may not be a good address in a good city 20 years from now.

THE OPERATING HOURS COMPARISON

McDonald's: 18 hours a day. some locations 24 hours. someone is always there. someone is always managing. something is always happening that requires a person to be physically present and making decisions.

6am opening crew. 11am lunch rush. 2pm lull. 5pm dinner rush. 9pm closing crew. 11pm cleaning. 12am close.

every day.

365 days a year.

including thanksgiving.

including christmas morning.

including the day after a pipe bursts in the kitchen at 3am and someone has to deal with it.

my portfolio: the pages run 24 hours a day.

the other 1,418 minutes the portfolio operates without me.

the portfolio does not require my presence to operate.

it requires my presence to improve.

those are different things.

and the difference is worth understanding precisely.

THE HEALTH INSPECTION COMPARISON

McDonald's: the county health department shows up unannounced.

they check the refrigerator temperatures.

they check the handwashing compliance.

they check the food storage dates.

they check the surface sanitization.

they check the pest control documentation.

they give you a letter grade that gets posted in the window where every customer can see it.

a C grade costs you customers.

a closure costs you everything.

my portfolio: gumroad does not conduct health inspections.

claude does not have a grease trap.

the scheduling tool has never been cited for improper food storage.

the couch has never received a letter grade.

i have never once in 19 months of running this operation worried about whether the county health department was going to show up and find something wrong.

i have never once worried about a surprise inspection of any kind from any authority.

the closest thing to an inspection my operation faces is a gumroad review from a buyer who didn't find the product as useful as they expected.

the response costs me $37-$77.

the review goes from 2 stars to 4 stars 70% of the time.

the health inspection equivalent costs me $37 and 10 minutes.

THE SCALABILITY COMPARISON

McDonald's: to double revenue you need a second location.

second location requires another $1,300,000.

another 50 employees.

another lease.

another set of equipment.

another health inspection regime.

another 18-hour operating day.

scaling a McDonald's operation means multiplying every cost, every complexity, and every operational burden by the number of locations you want to run.

the 10-location operator has 500 employees, 10 leases, 10 times the equipment, 10 times the regulatory burden, and a management structure that has become its own full-time business.

my portfolio: to double revenue i build 14 more pages.

each page costs one tuesday afternoon and a claude subscription i'm already paying for.

i hire one more VA at $420 a month per page.

no additional lease.

no additional equipment.

no additional health inspection.

no additional $1,300,000.

the marginal cost of doubling my operation is approximately $5,880 a month in additional VA costs and one afternoon per page of build time.

the marginal revenue of doubling my operation is approximately $492,000 more per year.

the scaling curve on a page portfolio is not linear.

it's closer to linear plus the compounding effect of a larger review ecosystem feeding cross-page discovery and a more sophisticated VA infrastructure that gets more efficient with each page added to it.

the scaling curve on a McDonald's franchise is linear in cost and sublinear in return because each additional location faces diminishing returns as the operator's attention gets spread thinner and the management overhead grows faster than the revenue.
