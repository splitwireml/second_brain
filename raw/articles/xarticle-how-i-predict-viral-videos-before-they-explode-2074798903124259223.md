## Step 1 - The Research Engine (this is the whole game)

This step is 80% of the result. Skip it and the other three steps produce polished videos nobody watches.

I don't ask "what should I post." I ask "what is already outperforming, and why."

Here is the exact method.

---

Build a swipe feed

Track 40 faceless channels in one lane (AI stories, history facts, motivation edits, pet clips, whatever you ship). Open a Google Sheet. Every day, log any video that broke out.

---

Score the outliers

An outlier is a video pulling far more views than the channel's own average. The math:

outlier score = video views ÷ channel median views

A channel that averages 20,000 views drops a video at 900,000. Score of 45. That video found something. Above 10 is a signal. Above 30 is a format you copy this week.

Ignore the raw view count. A 2M-view video on a 5M-average channel taught you nothing. A 900k-view video on a 20k channel is a map.

---

Separate topic from format

The topic is what the video is about. The format is the shape it's built in. Topics die in a week. Formats repeat for months.

Example. "POV: you're the last human on Earth" hits. The topic is loneliness. The format is "POV: you're the last X in Y." That format reskins into 200 videos: last knight, last barista, last dragon, last dial-up user. One outlier, a season of content.

Read the first 2 seconds

The hook does most of the work. For every outlier, log two things: the exact first frame and the first line of text on screen. That is what stopped the scroll. The rest of the video only has to not disappoint.

---

Feed the pattern to Claude

Paste 20 outlier titles and hooks into Claude and let it extract the shape, then generate new ones on your subject.

Prompt for pattern extraction:

```
You are a short-form video strategist.

Below are 20 videos that outperformed their channel by 10x or more.
For each one I've listed: the title, the first line of on-screen text,
and the outlier score.

[paste your 20 rows]

Do three things:
1. Group them into 5 repeatable FORMATS (the shape, not the topic).
2. For each format, write the hook rule in one sentence.
3. Rank the 5 formats by how easily they reskin to a new subject.

No general advice. Only patterns you can see in this exact data.
```

Prompt for idea generation:

```
Take format #1 from your last answer.
My subject is [your niche: e.g. ancient Rome facts].

Generate 30 video concepts in that exact format.
For each: the on-screen hook line (max 8 words), and the one
image or moment that carries the first 2 seconds.

Rank them 1-30 by scroll-stopping power. Cut anything generic.
```

Now you have 30 concepts that share a proven skeleton. You didn't guess once.

## Step 2 - Formats That Keep Hitting

You will see the same shapes surface every month. Log them once, reuse forever.

> POV: you're the [role] when [event]
Puts the viewer inside the scene in 1 line.

What if [familiar thing] but [twist]
Curiosity gap, no context needed.

The invisible-force gag
Objects move on their own, a chaotic reveal at the end.

Oddly satisfying process
A start-to-finish build or transformation, no talking.

1 vs escalation
1 vs 100, level 1 vs level 100, small stake to huge stake.

The creature reveal
A normal scene, then the last 2 seconds flip it.

Day in the life of [unexpected subject]
A rock, a medieval peasant, a deep-sea fish.

Each of these carried a different subject to millions last month. The shape is the reusable part. Your subject is the variable you swap in.

## Step 3 - Claude Writes the Script for Shots, Not Prose

Once a concept is picked, Claude writes to the format, not to a blog post. It writes for the cut.

Prompt for a short script:

```
You are writing a 25-second vertical short in the format:
"POV: you're the last lighthouse keeper on Earth."

Write for shots, not paragraphs. Output:
- on-screen hook text for the first 2 seconds (max 8 words)
- 5 shots, one line each, described for AI image generation
- one line of narration per shot, spoken pace
- the final 2-second beat that makes people rewatch or comment

Tone: quiet, eerie, no filler. End on an open loop, not a bow.
```

Claude questions the gaps if the concept is thin, then hands you a shot list you can build directly.

---

## Step 4 - CapCut Builds the Video

CapCut's AI tools turn the shot list into the finished vertical. Generate the frames, animate them, drop in AI voice and captions, export 9:16.

Instruction for the build:

```
Build a 25-second vertical (9:16) short from this 5-shot list.
Generate each shot as a moving clip, 5 seconds each, back to back.
Add the hook text over shot 1 for the first 2 seconds, large, high contrast.
Auto-caption the narration, word-by-word highlight, bottom third.
Score it with a low, tense ambient bed that never covers the voice.
Hold the final shot on the open loop. Export for TikTok and Shorts.
```

Lock your recurring characters and settings as assets so a one-off clip becomes a repeatable series. Cast once, change the plot, ship forever.

## Step 5 - Make Publishes and Tracks What Hit

The research loop only compounds if you feed your own results back into it. Make closes the loop.

The scenario runs on a schedule:

Trigger: new finished video in a Google Drive folder

1. Read the concept, format tag, and hook line from the intake row

2. Post the vertical to TikTok, YouTube Shorts, and Instagram Reels

3. Write titles and tags from the format tag

4. Wait 48 hours, pull view counts back via each platform's API

5. Write the new outlier score into the same sheet you research from

6. Flag any of your own videos that beat your median by 10x

7. Send a Telegram ping: which format won this week

Now your own hits become inputs. The formats that work for your account rise to the top. The factory learns.

Setup time: 4 hours once. Runs indefinitely after.
