# How I got my SaaS cited by ChatGPT in 30 days using Reddit comments

How I got my SaaS cited by ChatGPT in 30 days using Reddit comments

ChatGPT didn't mention my product 30 days ago. I would ask it for the best tools in my category and get the same list of big names every time, and I wasn't on it.

Today it cites my SaaS for multiple keywords, and the only thing I changed was posting helpful comments in the right Reddit threads.

The hard part is knowing which threads are the right ones, and that part can be done manually, for free, in a couple of hours. I'll show you both the manual way and the way I did it.

## 1. Why AI assistants quote Reddit more than any other website

When someone asks ChatGPT "what's the best CRM for a small agency", that answer has to come from somewhere.

Semrush analyzed which domains LLMs cite most often in their answers. Reddit came out #1, ahead of Wikipedia, ahead of YouTube, ahead of every major publication.

There are three reasons for this:

- Reddit is structured exactly like the questions people ask AI: someone asks "best X for Y", strangers with no commercial interest answer in detail, and the best answers get upvoted

- Reddit licensed its content to Google in 2024, so threads now rank on page 1 for almost every "best tool" query, which puts them directly in the retrieval path of AI assistants that browse the web

- Models treat upvoted opinions from anonymous users as more trustworthy than anything a brand says about itself

This matters because it completely flips where the leverage is. Most founders are trying to get their own website cited by AI, which is slow and depends on authority they don't have. Meanwhile, the most influential page about their category is probably a Reddit thread they've never commented on.

## 2. How one comment travels from Reddit to a ChatGPT answer?

The full chain looks like this:

1. Someone asks on Reddit: "what's the best tool for [your category]?"

2. The thread ranks on page 1 of Google for that keyword

3. AI assistants ingest the thread, both through training data and through live retrieval when they browse

4. A user asks ChatGPT or Perplexity the same question

5. The products mentioned in that thread become part of the answer

One comment works on three timelines at once: Redditors see it immediately, Google searchers find it for months, and AI assistants keep citing it long after you posted it.

At some point during this experiment it clicked for me: I wasn't doing Reddit marketing. I was doing SEO, except the website I was optimizing for was Reddit.

## 3. Finding the threads AI actually reads

You don't need 1,000 comments. You need around 100 in the right places, and the filter for "right place" is simple: the thread already ranks on Google. If Google surfaces it, the AI reads it.

Step 1: list 10 keywords with buyer intent. The searches your customers actually type: "best [category] tool", "[competitor] alternative", "how to [problem you solve]".

Step 2: find the Reddit threads ranking for them. Google each keyword with the site operator:

> best crm for small agency site:reddit.com

Every result is a thread that already ranks, which means it's already in the AI's reading list. The first 10 results per keyword are your targets.

Step 3: put it all in a spreadsheet. Thread URL, keyword, Google position, date of last activity. 10 keywords times 10 threads gives you 100 opportunities. This took me about 30min.

Step 4: comment over 30 days, not 3. I posted 3 to 4 comments a day, around 20 minutes of work. Posting 100 comments in a weekend from a single account is the fastest way to get flagged by Reddit's spam filters and lose everything.

To make this weekly instead of a one-off, I use my own tool, [RedShip](https://redship.io/): it finds the Reddit threads ranking on Google for my keywords and sends me a fresh batch every Monday morning. The manual version above gives you the exact same data for free, it just costs you the searching time.

## 4. The comment structure that doesn't get removed

This is where most founders fail. They find the perfect thread, drop a link to their product, and the comment is gone within the hour. Often shadow-removed, so they never even know.

After 100 comments, here's the structure that survives:

1. Answer the actual question first. The person asked something. Give them a real, complete answer, the kind you'd write if your product didn't exist. This should be 80% of the comment.

2. Mention your product next to competitors, never alone. "I've used [Competitor A] and [Competitor B], and I'm building [my product], here's how they differ" reads like a person. "Check out [my product]" reads like an ad.

3. Disclose that it's yours. Counterintuitive, but "full disclosure, this one is mine" performs better on Reddit than pretending to be a random user. Redditors detect astroturfing instantly and reward honesty with upvotes.

4. Match the depth of the thread. If the top comment is a 300-word comparison, a 2-line plug looks lazy next to it.

In practice, a comment looks something like this:

> Depends on what you're optimizing for. I used [Competitor A]
for a year, it's solid but gets expensive once you pass [limit].
[Competitor B] is cheaper but the [feature] is weak.

A few things that matter more than the tool: [genuine advice
specific to their question, 3-4 sentences].

Full disclosure, I also build [product], which focuses on
[specific difference]. Happy to answer questions on any of
these, I've spent way too much time in this space.

The test I run before posting:

If my product didn't exist, would this comment still be worth posting? If yes, it goes up. If no, I rewrite it.

There's a bonus effect to this format. Balanced, comparative comments are exactly what LLMs prefer to cite, because they look like data instead of advertising. The same structure that keeps moderators happy is the one that gets you into AI answers.

## 5. What happened after 30 days

ChatGPT now cites my product for my keywords, and AI search is now 2% of my traffic per month.

Three things stood out:

1. The ranking threads did all the heavy lifting. Comments on fresh, non-ranking threads got upvotes and a few visits, then died. Comments on page 1 threads kept driving traffic weeks later, and those are the only ones that showed up in AI answers.

2. AI citations lag, then compound. Nothing happened for the first weeks. Then mentions appeared in Perplexity first, because it retrieves live, and ChatGPT followed.

3. Old threads are not dead threads. Some of my best performing comments are on threads from 2024 and 2025. Reddit threads don't decay like social posts. If it ranks, it's alive.

To measure this, I check my AI mentions weekly with [Rankr](https://rankr.so/), a second tool I built that asks ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude and Perplexity about my keywords and logs whether my brand appears. The manual version: ask each AI your 10 keywords once a week and note the results in your spreadsheet.

## 6. "Isn't this just spam?" I hear you scream

Every comment in this system answers a question someone explicitly asked. Nobody is being interrupted or cold-messaged, and Reddit's moderators remove low-effort promotion fast, so a comment only survives if real people found it useful.

Where it does become spam is automation. AI-generated replies posted by bot accounts at scale is the thing Reddit is actively purging, and they do it retroactively: when an account gets banned, months of its comments disappear overnight, along with all the rankings and citations attached to them.

The searching and tracking can be automated. The writing can't, and shouldn't be.

## 7. What I'd tell someone starting today

If you take just one thing from this article, make it this: the Reddit threads ranking on Google for your keywords are the reading list of every AI assistant. You can get on that list this week, for free, with a Google operator and a spreadsheet.

A few more things I'd pass on:

- Start with 10 keywords, not 50. Depth beats coverage, and 100 good comments is already a month of work

- Write every comment yourself. 20 minutes a day is the entire cost of this channel

- Be patient: Reddit reacts in days, Google in weeks, AI in months, and the AI layer is the one that compounds

Everyone is still fighting over Google rankings while almost nobody is deliberately working on what ChatGPT says about their category. We're still early.
