# 12 GTM Engineering Workflows That Drive Revenue (Full Breakdown)

12 GTM Engineering Workflows That Drive Revenue (Full Breakdown)

We've generated $30M+ in pipeline across 10M+ cold emails.

One building materials client generated $3.7M in pipeline from just two of these workflows: cold email and CRM re-engagement.

All 12 are mapped end to end, and 7 of them share the exact same four steps:

→ Find the buying committee
→ Verify every email twice
→ Score fit and timing
→ Call the best accounts, email the rest

Build those four once, and every new workflow is just a new trigger plugged into the same system.

If you run sales or marketing at a B2B company, these are the 12 I'd build first.
Each one comes with the exact tools it runs on and a link to the full playbook.

A quick note on scope.

GTM engineering covers the whole revenue team: marketing, sales and customer success.

These 12 sit on the acquisition side, from first signal to booked meeting.
The same thinking runs onboarding handoffs, usage alerts and renewal risk on the customer side.

They go from warmest to coldest, which is also the order I'd build them in.

# Part 1: Start With Data You Already Own:

These four need no new list. The leads are already in your CRM, your inbox or your forms.

## 1. CRM Re-engagement:

Every closed-lost deal already cost you a demo, a proposal and weeks of a rep's time.

Somewhere in the call recording is the real reason they said no.
Budget, timing, a missing feature, the wrong person.

Claude Code reads the recordings in Gong and names the objection for each deal.
Then the workflow watches for the change that answers it:
→ Hiring or a tool change (Sumble)
→ Return visits to your site (RB2B)
→ A champion changing jobs (PredictLeads)

If the blocker hasn't moved, the deal stays parked and no credits get spent.
If it has, the buying committee gets re-enriched and every email verified twice.
Tier one gets a call. The rest get an email that opens on the objection.

This is the lowest hanging fruit on the list. You already paid to create these deals.

## 2. Champion Moves:

Someone who bought from you just started a new job.
New budget, new team, a plan to write in their first 90 days. And they already trust you.

The workflow keeps a list of everyone who bought from you or praised you on a call (HubSpot, Gong).
It checks that list every week for job changes (LinkedIn, Sumble).
A move only counts once a second source confirms it.

The new company gets scored on fit, and the new email gets found and verified twice.
Tier one also gets a mobile, and whoever owned the old account gets a Slack alert.
They send a personal note on LinkedIn in the first few weeks, then call.

Tiers two and three get a short email that opens on the work you did together.

Full playbook: [revgrowth.ai/playbooks/champion-moves](https://revgrowth.ai/playbooks/champion-moves?utm_source=x&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=gtm-workflows-article)

## 3. Reply to Warm Call:

An interested reply is the warmest lead outbound will ever give you, and it cools fast.

The moment a reply gets tagged interested in MasterInbox, four things happen:
→ A direct dial gets found (GetLeads, FullEnrich)
→ The account gets researched (Claude Code, Sumble)
→ The lead gets pushed to your CRM (OutboundSync, HubSpot)
→ The SDR gets a Slack ping

The SDR calls within minutes, while the prospect still remembers writing back.

Reason why this works: "sure, send me more info" is a maybe.
A call right after it turns the maybe into a meeting on the calendar.

Full playbook: [revgrowth.ai/playbooks/reply-to-warm-call](https://revgrowth.ai/playbooks/reply-to-warm-call?utm_source=x&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=gtm-workflows-article)

## 4. Form Fill to Call:

A demo request lands at 2:14pm. The rep sees it the next morning.
By then they've booked with someone else.

Every demo, contact and pricing form lands in the CRM with the page it came from.
The person and company get enriched with a direct dial, then scored:
→ Fits go to a rep now
→ Customers and open deals go to their owner
→ Non-fits get a useful answer instead of silence

The rep gets a Slack alert with who they are and what they asked for, and calls within five minutes.
No answer means five days of short follow-ups from the rep's own inbox.

Harvard Business Review looked at how fast firms respond to a web lead.
Firms that responded within an hour were nearly 7x more likely to qualify it than firms that waited an hour longer.

# Part 2: Catch Buyers Raising Their Hand:

These five watch the places buyers show intent in public: your website, LinkedIn and review sites.

## 5. Website De-anonymization:

Every day, buyers read your pricing page and leave without a word.

Visitor ID tools like RB2B put a name to roughly 1 in 10 to 1 in 5 of your US visitors.
A small slice, and still the warmest list you'll get, because they came to you.

The workflow names the company and the person, then checks the CRM so open deals and customers go to their owner.
Otherwise, it finds the rest of the buying committee and verifies every email twice.

Pricing-page and repeat visits, plus hiring and tool changes from Sumble, decide the tier.
Tier one reaches a rep within minutes through a Slack alert. Everyone else gets an evergreen email.

Full playbook: [revgrowth.ai/playbooks/website-de-anonymization](https://revgrowth.ai/playbooks/website-de-anonymization?utm_source=x&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=gtm-workflows-article)

## 6. LinkedIn Inbound:

Every connection request and profile visit is somebody checking you out.
The trick is only answering the ones who fit.

Each one gets enriched and checked against the CRM.
Then it's weighed with site visits (RB2B) and changes at their company (Sumble).
Jev and OpenRouter score fit and intent.

High-intent fits get a personal note.
Other fits get an opener sent through HeyReach, with a Slack ping when they reply.
Everyone else gets no message at all.

That last part matters. Skipping the wrong people keeps your account healthy and your replies worth reading.

Full playbook: [revgrowth.ai/playbooks/linkedin-inbound](https://revgrowth.ai/playbooks/linkedin-inbound?utm_source=x&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=gtm-workflows-article)

## 7. Competitor Followers:

Your competitor spent years building an audience of your buyers.
It sits on their LinkedIn company page, and you can pull the new followers every week.

The workflow drops the competitor's own employees, enriches the buying committee and verifies every email twice.

Then it stacks the signals:
→ They run the competitor's tool (Sumble)
→ They engage with its posts (Apify)
→ They visited your site (RB2B)

Fit and timing decide the tier.
Tier one gets a call and personal outreach on LinkedIn and Gmail. Tiers two and three get an email campaign.

Full playbook: [revgrowth.ai/playbooks/competitor-followers](https://revgrowth.ai/playbooks/competitor-followers?utm_source=x&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=gtm-workflows-article)

## 8. Content Engagement:

Your buyers comment on posts about the exact problem you solve, every day, with their name and company attached.

Apify scrapes three sources:
→ Posts on your topics
→ People engaging with your competitors' posts
→ People engaging with your own team's posts

A comment counts for more than a like.

The CRM gets checked, and the buying committee gets enriched and verified.
Hiring, stack changes and site visits get added on top.
After scoring, tier one gets a Slack alert and a call or personal note. Everyone else gets an evergreen email.

Full playbook: [revgrowth.ai/playbooks/content-engagement](https://revgrowth.ai/playbooks/content-engagement?utm_source=x&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=gtm-workflows-article)

## 9. Competitor Reviews:

Your competitor's 1-star reviews are a lead list.
The buyer wrote down the exact complaint, and some even listed their company size and job title.

Apify pulls the reviews from G2, Capterra and Trustpilot, and Serper finds the Reddit threads.
Claude Code groups the complaints into the few themes that keep coming back.
You keep only the ones you actually solve.

Sumble finds the companies running the competitor's tool.
Customers, open deals and active sequences come off the list.
Every account gets scored on fit and matched to the complaint that fits it best.

Then each one hears about that one complaint, and how you fix it.

# Part 3: Build New Pipeline at Scale:

These three create pipeline from scratch. They take more setup than the rest, and they scale the furthest.

## 10. Lookalike Audiences:

Your best customer is the best lead list you own.

Start from your best accounts in HubSpot and what you hear on calls in Gong. That becomes the ICP.
DiscoLike turns it into three lookalike lists, built from the websites of companies that already paid you.

Every account gets qualified against the ICP before anyone gets enriched.
Firecrawl reads the site, then Jev and OpenRouter score it.
So you only pay to find contacts at companies that fit.

Then it's enrich, verify twice, write the messaging, and reach out by tier.

Full playbook: [revgrowth.ai/playbooks/lookalike-audiences](https://revgrowth.ai/playbooks/lookalike-audiences?utm_source=x&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=gtm-workflows-article)

## 11. Cold Email:

This is the one that feeds everything else on the list.

Research comes first: call recordings, won and lost deals, customer interviews. That becomes the ICP.
The ICP becomes a mapped market from more than one source (DiscoLike, LinkedIn, Google Maps).
Then it's layered with signals, pain segments and your own first-party data.

Every account gets qualified, and every contact enriched through a four-vendor waterfall and verified twice.
The offer gets written per pain segment.
Replies route through Outboundsync into HubSpot with a Slack alert.

Then you test every week.

It compounds.
One client sells branded cups to coffee shops, bakeries and caterers.
In 16 months, 1M+ cold emails opened 4,313 qualified conversations for them.

Full playbook: [revgrowth.ai/playbooks/modern-outbound](https://revgrowth.ai/playbooks/modern-outbound?utm_source=x&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=gtm-workflows-article)

## 12. Cold Calling:

If you sell to owners who live on their phones (contractors, clinics, shops), email can't replace this channel.

The ICP comes from real conversations.
The list covers the whole market, pulled from DiscoLike and Google Maps and stacked with signals and your own data.
Every account gets qualified, then direct dials come from GetLeads, FullEnrich and BetterContact.

A parallel dialer (Salesfinity) works five lists at once:
→ The cold ICP list
→ Newsletter subscribers
→ Email replies
→ Site visitors
→ Missed deals

The rep only talks to people who pick up.
Meetings sync to HubSpot and run through your sales process to closed won.

# Where to Start:

Don't build all 12 at once. Here's the order I'd go in:

1. Build the four shared steps first: a contact waterfall, two email checks, a scoring prompt and a Slack alert.
2. Plug in the data you already own: CRM Re-engagement and Champion Moves.
3. Add speed: Reply to Warm Call and Form Fill to Call.
4. Add one public signal: Website De-anonymization if you have traffic, Competitor Reviews against a bigger name.
5. Then go net-new: Lookalike Audiences first, then Cold Email and Cold Calling.
