## here's what actually controls reply rate

reply rate is downstream of three things. in this order:

1. who's on the list
2. what you're offering them
3. who the message is from

after those three, copy starts to matter a little. volume is further down. tools are noise.

every agency you've paid sequenced this in reverse.

they spent your retainer rewriting subject lines and "personalisation tokens" while running it to a list that was never going to reply to anything.

---

## the list

when the niche is real, going narrower beats going bigger almost every time.

we ran a fractional cfo campaign recently.

2,961 inmails over 30 days. 6% reply rate. small.

but 26 interested leads turned into 4 closed deals - 15% close rate from cold outbound.

that didn't happen because the copy was sharp.

every name on the list was a founder running a freshly-funded seed-to-series-a saas company with no existing finance leadership.

those founders are looking for someone like our client right now. they're going to hire one this quarter whether they reply to us or not. our message just got there first.

most operators stop at "saas founders 50-500 employees."

that's literally just a guess.

the buyers inside that group are maybe 5% of the names. you're running outbound to 95% noise and blaming the copy when nobody replies.

---

## the offer

i spent 6 months optimising cold email copy. tested 100 angles. results barely moved.

then i changed the front-end offer instead of the message. that's when results moved.

cold traffic only replies in volume to two things.

one is a free sample of the thing they'd normally pay for. the other is solving their problem live on the call.

anything else is asking a stranger to bet 30 minutes on a maybe.

most founders default to that third option without realising it: "hop on a call to learn more." that's why your reply rate is where it is.

we ran a campaign for an ai automations agency that had been doing outbound for months with almost nothing to show for it.

we didn't change the channel. didn't change the volume. didn't change the targeting.

changed the offer from "discuss ai for your business" to "we'll build you a working ai automation, live on the first call, free."

result: 14 calls booked in 9 days at a 25.8% reply rate.

all from one change in what they were pitching.

---

## the sender

linkedin reply rates double when the account is a woman's instead of a man's. 

male decision makers click faster. female decision makers feel safer replying. enterprise especially.

a yelp exec replied to one of our inmails sent from an account belonging to someone making $45k/year.

morgan stanley booked a call through a profile named "elizabeth" with 77 connections.

---

## one cta change that doubled our reply rate

once the first three are sorted, copy is the next place to look.

we changed the cta on one campaign from "hop on a call" to "let me show you the fix on a call." reply rates doubled.

that's the entire change.

telling them what would actually happen on the call beat every version of the corporate intro paragraph we'd been sending. we never went back to the old wording on any campaign after that.

cold copy sweet spot: 50 to 80 words. plain text. no bold. no bullets. no logos. the more perfected it looks, the faster it gets deleted.

---

## the math

5,000 inmails a month per profile at a 9% reply rate is 450 replies per account per month.

run that across 4 profiles and you've got 1,800 conversations a month.

even at brutal qualification (say 10% of replies turn into booked, qualified calls) that's 180 calls.

you don't need a 50% reply rate to print.

you need a real list, a front-end offer that doesn't bore people, and the system to send at volume without getting your accounts killed.

a 20% reply rate is what the best campaigns hit. most of the money actually comes from campaigns running between 8 and 12%.

## what most people are actually doing wrong

a client came to us recently with 17 calls booked in 3 weeks. only 1 had closed. he wanted to rewrite the campaign.

the campaign was doing its job. the sales process wasn't. he was wasting 16 qualified leads on a close that couldn't hold them.

before you blame the channel, look at how many of your last 20 booked calls actually closed.

if that number is shit, leads were never the problem you needed to fix.

we pre-qualify every interested reply with a live setter before it hits the client's calendar. that turns 10% close rates into 30%+. by the time the founder is on the call, the prospect is already half-sold.
