# Creating Content Is Gasoline For Your Career (It's The New Resume)

Creating Content Is Gasoline For Your Career (It's The New Resume)

Going viral is a terrible reason to create content online.

Especially if you’re NOT looking to become an influencer, start a side hustle, or build a business.

But if you’re more worried about your career and getting:

- Laid off

- Denied a promotion

- Or passed over for raises

You should be publishing consistently on the internet.

But here’s what you may not already know:

## Writing online is actually the key to accelerating your career.

Because the traditional resume is dying.

For example:

Imagine you and an identical candidate are applying for a dream job. You show up with a master-crafted resume made by the best in the world. The other person has no resume but has been writing online for years.

You chuckle to yourself and think, "This job is mine!"

## But before you even get the chance to interview, they're offered the role (with a fat sign-on bonus).

Because in today's digital-first world, employers don't care:

- Where you went to school—they want to see you already doing the work.

- What you did 3 years ago—they want to see what insights you're learning about and sharing today.

- About fluffed-up "claims" on a resume—they want to see you build and showcase your experience in public.

But there are also SO MANY benefits beyond just the financial.

Here are 5 unexpected ways writing every day can lead to massive personal growth:

# 1. Give yourself accountability

Setting yourself a writing and publishing goal and sticking to it is transformational.

This kind of commitment is a promise to yourself. And as you build a consistent streak of keeping that promise, this starts to trickle into all other areas of your life:

- How you approach your nutrition

- Building more exercise into your routine

- Making extra time for important relationships

We see it over and over again in our writing programs—the writer’s act of showing up daily fosters a greater sense of accountability in other areas of their lives, too.

# 2. Get clarity on your feelings

Everyone talks about how writing leads to clarity of thought.

What doesn't get talked about is how writing leads to clarity of emotion. When you write about a topic, you discover how you feel about it and why it matters to you personally. This motivates you to write about it more (and so reinforces your writing habit) or shows you which topics just aren’t as important to you as you previously thought.

In short, it creates and reinforces your worldview.

I call this listening for internal “signal,” and it’s just as critical to finding your way as a writer as the feedback you get from your readers.

# 3. Create your writing “voice”

So many writers stress over "finding their voice."

But when you write a lot, you are actively creating your voice. Over time, as if by magic, your voice appears on the page in front of you. The most exciting part of this process, though, is you can be conscious in the creation process: experiment with different voices and blend different archetypes until you find one that suits you.

4. Build your self-confidence

If you’re socially anxious or insecure (and everyone is to some extent), writing and publishing every day is one of the most powerful ways to build self-confidence.

When you write, you go through a three-stage process:

1. You practice your ideas, beliefs, and convictions.

2. The more you write, the better you get at articulating them

3. As you get better at articulating them, you learn to trust yourself

This has a massive impact across all areas of your life: at work, at home, and in your relationships.

# 5. Practice patience

Writing takes time because thinking takes time.

Writing is not just about putting pretty (or smart) words on the page. It's about stacking thoughts to form a perspective. Sometimes, you won’t get it right the first time. So you need to practice patience.

When you can learn to be patient with yourself, your thoughts, and your writing, it's much easier to be patient with others and theirs.
