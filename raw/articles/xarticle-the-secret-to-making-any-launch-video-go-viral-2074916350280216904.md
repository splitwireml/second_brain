# The Secret to Making Any Launch Video Go Viral

The Secret to Making Any Launch Video Go Viral

I used to make YouTube videos that did BILLIONS of views a month. Now i make tech companies go viral.

Our videos average over a million views.

Years of creating content and chasing numbers has helped me get here.

I put down all my learnings to create a list of things you can do in a launch video go viral (predictably).

This document is that exact list. No gatekeeping. 

PRO TIP: feed this entire article to claude and ask fable create an checklist for launch videos.

Everything written below is a rule I personally tested on a launch that got millions of views. Steal them.

#1 EDITORS LIST

1. You need to speed up the final cut by about 10% before launching the video.

This is what we do for every launch.

And trust me, no client has caught it, and neither have you.

it makes the video measurably more engaging, attention spans these days demand this.

I would not push it more than 10% though, if you push it to 15 or 20% then it’s hard to follow and process the information.

10 is the sweet spot.

2. Keep the Video Moving

If you have a flat 2D screenshot for ten seconds while a voiceover talk…IT WILL GET SKIPPED, even when what you're saying is good.

Sometimes, even our clients suggest talking about important info over a frame for more than 10 seconds but we always push back. 

The goal is to go VIRAL. Static, inanimate frames kill the video.

Everything important can be written in text instead.

Keep the camera alive at all times: even a boring UI scene should get a zoom, a push, a pan.

3. Don't tell stories.

This is a hot take but you don’t have time to weave a story in your launch.

Setting up a story and making someone care takes fifteen seconds minimum, and you have five.

Story is a luxury that assumes a captive audience.

On a feed, nobody is captive. Lead with the payoff.

4. There should only be one idea per scene

Sync the voiceover to the visual so tightly, you should be able to mute the video and still roughly follow it.

You live inside your product. The viewer doesn’t.

The viewer just arrived.

If a 12 year old can't tell you what happened in the first ten seconds, assume that the market can't either.

#2 THE PROOF WINDOW (seconds 5 to 30)

5. Never use jargon

"Save hours." "Boost productivity." "The all-in-one platform for whatever."

Think about what you do when an obvious advertisement crosses your feed.

You skip it, right?

It’s the same with launch videos. If only you can understand what the video is talking about, chances are, no one else will watch past the first few seconds.

6. Pick your “AHA” moments and build around them

The “aha” moment is the two seconds where the viewer goes "oh, that's actually insane."

Ask yourself this

What is the single most impressive thing my product does, and can I show it?

7. Prove it on screen.

Screen share the actual product.

One time for an AI agent we run a split screen

The interface on one side visibly doing something, the code on the other, the agent clearly working.

The viewer needs to see it, not take your word for it.

8. Always lead with the proof.

One of our launch videos opens on a hard number,

"$500 million in revenue," then cuts straight into the demo.

If you have to tell people it's great, it isn't.

#3 HOOK ENGINEERING

9. 99% of the launch is the hook

There’s a drop in viewers after the first 5 seconds, it's called a retention cliff.

Your goal should be to have a hook so good that the cliff is a straight line

On a strong two minute launch video, holding 30 to 50% of viewers past the hook is a good result, and roughly half of those stay to the end.

10. Put the wildest moment of the entire video first.

Here’s a list of things you should NOT open your video with

> a logo animation 
> a cinematic establishing shot 
> twelve seconds of the founder talking 

You should preload your video with the craziest moment to stop the scroll and get people watching for longer.

11. Attention spans are cooked

Ten years ago a 15 second hook was fine.

TikTok and Reels have trained everyone to be hooked in five seconds or be gone, and that time frame keeps dropping every year ... we're planning for a future where it's one or two seconds.

Hook them in five to seven, or nothing else on this list matters.

#4 THE PREP

12. You should have a clear demo of your product and a solid answer to these questions before starting on the video

What makes you different from the obvious competitor?

Is there a backend or process we can visualize to prove something real is happening?

What can we actually show?

13. Write the script around one non negotiable: the customer’s desired outcome

A sharp, concrete setup that drops you straight into the stakes.

"You're an AI company making $5M a month and you want to get to $20M."

Then immediately into how.

14. Design the entire video before you animate a single frame.

Storyboard in Figma. real UI, branding, and assets.

Lay out style frames scene by scene.

You don't animate from scratch and hope.

The full stack we use: Figma, Frame, After Effects, Premiere Pro, Photoshop, FL Studio.

THE BONUS RULE (the one most studios won't do)

15. A launch video goes through around ten full versions before the launch.

Ten rounds of feedback on the entire stitch.

We will throw out a scene we loved, redesign it, and reanimate it the moment it stops making sense for the average viewer 

We pull all-nighters to get these launches right and it’s what gives that extra 1% to get it over the line.
