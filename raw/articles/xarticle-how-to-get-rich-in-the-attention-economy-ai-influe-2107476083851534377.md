## the business in one line

create an AI influencer, use it to make ads, sell those ads to brands

that's it... the rest of this article is the plumbing

the reason it works is boring and real: an ad dies the more people see it

Meta calls it creative fatigue, when an audience has seen the same creative too many times they engage less and the cost per result goes up

so a DTC brand running paid social is always hungry for new creatives, and filming real creators every week is slow and expensive

you show up with a character that never gets tired, never books out, and can be dropped into any winning format

the offer:

- a steady stream of creatives every month

- one character the audience starts to recognize

- formats copied from what's already winning in their niche

- a $1,500 to $10,000 monthly retainer depending on volume

the brand buys a refill schedule, you sell them the refills

## find what's going viral with Apify and Scrape Creators

everything starts with research, because you never invent a format when the feed already tells you what works

pick ONE product niche first... skincare, supplements, pet stuff, whatever brands you want to pitch actually sell

then point your scrapers at TikTok and Instagram:

- Apify's TikTok trends scraper pulls trending hashtags, songs, creators and videos per country

- Apify's TikTok and Instagram hashtag scrapers grab top videos for your niche hashtags with views, shares and comments

- Apify's Instagram reel scraper takes a reel link and returns the caption, transcript and the video itself

- Scrape Creators gives you the TikTok trending feed, keyword and top search, and Instagram trending reels

rank everything by traction, plays and shares first, then comments

two warnings before you trust the numbers... Scrape Creators says TikTok and Instagram can return duplicates, so dedupe by link, and Instagram play counts can be off and only count Instagram views, not the Facebook cross-post

the winners go into a vault (i keep mine in Obsidian) and every entry gets three things:

- the link

- the hook, word for word

- the beat map, what happens second by second

this is where research usually goes to waste, so here's the split:

capture record: the link, the views, the date, proof it worked

persuasion record: the hook, the beats, the product moment, WHY it worked

the capture record tells you a video won, the persuasion record is the part you can actually reuse with your character

## build your influencer from scratch

now you need the face that will star in every ad

start on Pinterest... search the vibe your niche buys from, the gym girl, the skincare nerd, the outdoorsy dad, and save the references into one board

you're looking for a pattern in faces, hair, wardrobe and setting, then you build one person out of it

the character sheet lives in Nano Banana Pro, because Google built it to hold the resemblance of up to five characters and the fidelity of up to fourteen objects in one workflow

build it in this order:

1. a headshot first, neutral light, plain background, no text

2. a full body shot generated from that headshot so the face carries over

3. one closed wardrobe line, a short list of outfits and nothing outside it

4. one identity anchor, a mark or detail that shows up in every shot

5. phone-photo realism rules, slight grain, imperfect framing, real rooms, no studio gloss

the closed wardrobe matters more than it looks... when the outfit drifts between ads, the audience reads it as a different person

the identity anchor is your check, if it's missing from a frame the frame goes in the bin

a character sheet is a contract with your future self, every ad gets judged against it

## Higgsfield AI Influencer and Genjutsu

the first is a zero-prompt builder... every attribute comes from menus, 19 settings in the updated version, and characters render at up to 4K

one generation gives you a close-up portrait and a full-body shot of the same character, which is basically a starter character sheet in one click

to hold that face across a lot of posts, Higgsfield's own guide adds a Soul ID step: train it on 20 or more photos of the character and use that identity for everything after

so your character library ends up as a set of locked identities you can pull into any job

then Genjutsu, which lives in the Motion tab and does the trend copying:

- Motion Transfer pulls the movement out of a reference video and your character performs it frame for frame

- Object Swap replaces one element in the clip, a product, an outfit or a prop, and keeps the rest of the footage

- each generation takes one reference video of 4 to 30 seconds and up to 30 images of characters, products or clothes

- 30+ trending presets if you'd rather start from a format than a reference

here's the move with your vault: take a winner from the persuasion record, feed it to Motion Transfer, and your influencer replaces the original creator doing the exact same beats

then Object Swap drops the client's product into the hand where the old product was

and you get batch variations out of the same performance... reuse it across different characters, outfits, locations or styles without filming each version separately

## or create your own with Seedance 2.5

copying trends gets you in the door, original formats keep the retainer alive

Seedance 2.5 is where i make those... clips up to 30 seconds, any aspect ratio from 9:16 to 21:9, audio generated in the same pass

it takes up to 50 references, and practitioners start the same way i do, in image, with a turnaround character sheet on white, then tag those images into the video prompt

the rules i follow for original ads:

- script math first, 3.5 words per second of spoken line

- short clips, one beat per generation, stitched after

- hook first, render the opening line before anything else

- 1080p for anything that ships to a client

the word math is the one people skip... a script that's too long for the clip gets rushed and the character sounds like a robot reading terms of service

one honest caveat: an independent review warns that a showcase doesn't prove repeatable character or product consistency, and limits change by provider and plan

so test YOUR character across a batch of renders before you promise a brand anything

## build examples and pitch your first brand

this is the step that turns a cool workflow into money

pick a real DTC brand in your niche, one that's already running ads, and make them a few spec ads with your character holding their product

spec ads beat a pitch deck, because the brand sees their own product in a format that's already winning on their feed

the pitch, in plain words:

"here are a few ads i made for you with my AI influencer, i can send you a steady stream of these every month - $1,500 to $10,000 a month depending on how many you need"

the volume argument writes itself, the refresh benchmarks floating around are every 2 to 4 weeks on Meta and weekly on TikTok

there's a second revenue line once your account grows: sell ad spots on the influencer's own account to brands

then disclosure, which you never skip:

- TikTok requires an AI-generated label on realistic AI content

- TikTok ads with undisclosed AI content get rejected or restricted

- branded posts on TikTok need the commercial content disclosure toggle on

- Meta wants paid partnerships tagged with the business partner

- the FTC says disclose the brand connection with the post itself

the FTC also says not to assume the platform's tool is enough by itself, so add your own clear line too

label everything, a banned account kills the retainer faster than any bad ad
