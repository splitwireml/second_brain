## So how do you build a $100K SaaS?

There are a few components at play here: Product, Marketing, and an edge.
I started with the edge.

Back in 2024 (until today), there was no really good open-source solution for social media scheduling. And it's ridiculous, because there is an open-source solution for everything.

Postiz was the only real open-source project offering robust social media scheduling, and because it was so desired (keep in mind it was before Vibe Coding), there was a ton of demand for it.

Every 2-3 weeks, I would post a new version of my open-source project on Reddit /r/selfhosted, and every time the post would get to 250k+ views. Now you would probably say - developers don't pay money, and you are absolutely right, they don't.

I don't need them to pay me; I want them to use the free, open-source software, talk about it, build on it, and tell others. I was always a branding person. When you have a strong name, there is no stronger marketing than that.

They helped me to build the product, contributed code, and gave me fast feedback on everything they needed.

First post: https://www.reddit.com/r/selfhosted/comments/1f4x806/postiz_opensource_social_media_scheduling_tool/

The first $1k MRR was too fast, so we were stuck at $3k MRR. I think this is where many founders get stuck. You see, I had months where almost all my customers changed, and new ones came in. I kept on $3k MRR, but it was new people; I just couldn't push it.

## Product

No matter how good your distribution is, if you have a bad product, you can keep bringing customers in all the time, but they will churn.

If you post something and it doesn't post. You have duplicate posts; the editor is not comfortable, and you won't win the game. You will stagnate or die.

You can't just market forever, especially if you sell a monthly plan.
So I registered all my competitors who won and started to see "what I like" and "what I don't like". I contacted my designer, and we came up with a new design that incorporates the good things from the other products and addresses the improvements they need.

AI coding just started, and I know that making stuff look not "AI Vibe-coded" is the key. This is why, until today, I use designers for everything.

Except for the frontend, I also felt the backend was lagging behind. I had some common architecture with BullMQ and Redis that was just too hard to maintain, so I moved to Temporal, which helped me fix and find tons of bugs and edge cases.

You cannot believe how much reliability is important for such a tool, uptime monitors, tests, observability - it's a must, you have a small bug that one post does not go out, and people will churn.

## Marketing

Distribution is key in 2026. Most people don't understand it.
My goal, as head of growth at my previous company, was to understand how to get fast wins without building them up over time.

I have "some" following here on X, but I never really paid too much attention to it. Because I didn't want to play a long game I might lose, I preferred to play small games I could win or lose.

If you actually think about it, there are tons of people everywhere, and you need to find a way to get their eyeballs to your products. The more people who need your product and get exposed to it, the better.

People are everywhere, and the only thing that stops you from getting to them is algorithms. Like, imagine every post you posted would be seen by 1 million people every time? Wouldn't you make much more money with your SaaS?

It's just an algorithm - that's it. Can you find platforms that give you more traffic without preparation? You absolutely can. Traffic is everywhere; there are billions of people in the world, you just need to investigate.

Back then, I understood that every time I posted on Reddit, if I got 2-3 friends to upvote my post, the algorithm would expose me to a lot more people.

Then I saw the same thing on TikTok, for example: https://www.tiktok.com/@getpostiz/video/7575210795907222815

You can have no followers and still get tons of traffic.
Later, I saw that the articles on X are also getting a lot of traffic because the platform's founders wanted to push long-form.

Product Hunt launch? I launched 4 times, every time, I would do a massive outreach to win (and won every time),

Then I discovered Lemmy (it's like Reddit), and every one of my posts would get 100 upvotes every time.

And I would absolutely not try to push platforms like YouTube videos (not shorts), as I've seen it's almost impossible to get good growth on.
