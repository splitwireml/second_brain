# If I had $7.77 to my name and wanted to make $10,000 this summer, here's what I'd do

If I had $7.77 to my name and wanted to make $10,000 this summer, here's what I'd do

You don’t need a marketing degree. 

You don’t need a massive following. 

And following this exact setup, creators I’ve trained have gone from zero experience to $5K+ months within a few weeks. 

Here’s my system:

---

## Step 1: Understand the model

1.1 - How you get paid 

Before you film a video you need to understand how the money works in tech UGC. 

Tech companies (apps, SaaS, AI tools) pay creators on retainer to post short-form content on TikTok and Instagram from brand owned accounts. You get a base pay per month usually, then most companies layer on view bonuses that pay you a CPM (cost per 1k views) every time a video performs well. 

One viral video can pay you more than entire months salary at your 9-5 job. 

I had a video hit a couple million views in my 2nd month and got an $800 bonus payout for content that took me maybe 20 minutes to make.

1.2 - Why tech specifically 

Not all UGC is created equal. 

Product UGC pays one-off fees and has almost no upside, wouldn’t recommend. 

Tech UGC is retainer based so you have a monthly income you can depend on. 

And a lot of these tech companies are sitting on VC funding which means their budgets for content are significantly higher than some random dropshipping brand. 

I trained a creator who’s first video hit 800k views and she made $1.2K with no experience. 

All of this is 100% possible for you.

---

## Step 2: Workflow to land client #1

2.1 - Learn what performs before you pitch a brand 

Most try to land a client first and then figure out content strategy but it’s backwards. 

Before reaching out to a brand, spend time studying what works in different niches. 

Open TikTok and IG, search for apps that are in a category you’re already interested in, like study apps or fitness trackers, then sort the content by most popular. 

Watch the 15-30 top videos from the last 2 months and analyze what they all have in common… hooks, pacing, how they show the product, length, tone, CTAs, etc. 

Write it all down. 

2.2 - Build your pitch and land client #1 

Tech companies post listings for “viral content creator” roles on job boards, twitter, and in creator communities all the time. 

You don’t need a portfolio to start. 

Just prove you understand short-form content and you’ve studied their niche. 

When you apply, reference specific videos from their competitors that did well. Tell them what you noticed about the format, why it worked, and how you’d iterate it for them. 

That’ll immediately separate you from the 95% of AI proposals they get. 

If you want to skip the cold outreach phase, you can join communities like CreatorEra that specifically connect trained creators with brands that are actively hiring. 

You’ll get matched with these brands, receive strategy training before you start, and go into your first role with a genuine understanding for what you’re doing. 

2.3 - Nail the first 72 hours on a new account 

Once you land your first client, they’ll send you a brief… basically a short doc explaining their brand, audience, competitors, and examples of content that’s worked before. 

Then you create your accounts on IG and TikTok with a new email. 

Set up your profile with a real photo of yourself, add “[brand] ambassador” to your bio, and then spend the first 3 days warming up the account before you post anything. 

Follow other creators in the niche, interact with videos, and search for relevant topics because the algorithm needs to understand what you’re doing before it decides who to show your videos to. 

This matters a lot more for TikTok than IG.

2.4 - Your first 10 videos should be proven formats only 

Don’t use your first 10 videos for experimentation… fresh accounts get pushed into the algorithm even more than seasoned accounts. 

It has no idea if you’re a nobody or a celebrity who just signed up so it’ll give you a wider audience than it normally would later on. 

Take the best videos you found in your research from 2.1 

Then recreate them exactly with same hook, pacing, screen layout, and energy from the original video. Don’t worry about getting called out for “stealing,” it’s normal. 

2.5 - Post consistently and cross-post 

Two posts per day is the sweet post for the algo to consistently push your content. 

Every video you make should be cross-posted on TikTok, IG Reels, YouTube Shorts, and FB… Each platform is another opportunity to go viral and get paid more. 

2.6 - Review your content religiously 

After your first week, look at your retention data. 

If you still have 70% of viewers watching at the 3 second mark, then you know your hook is working and you can replicate that in the rest of your content. 

If a hook is performing, make 14 variations of videos with the same hook and only change the last half og your video until you find the perfect viral formula. 

After two weeks, you should gain some traction, but if not, scrap the strategy and start over with a new approach based on what your competitors are doing right now.

## Why this works

Tech UGC works because it’s the intersection of a high-paying industry and a content format that doesn’t require your to have a following or years of experience. 

Tech companies have the funding to pay their creators well. 

The only barrier to entry is your willingness to study what works, apply it to the opportunities, and post consistently once you land some clients. 

If you think this won’t work, take another look at the $7.77 in your bank account. 

And remember it COULD be $10,000 in a couple months if you kill your limitations.
