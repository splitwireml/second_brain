GPT-6 Astra Has Completely Changed The Way I Run My Business. Here’s Why.

OpenAI’s latest model Astra is absurd.

I’ve spent the last 10 days maxing out my usage limits on the $200/mo plan, and I’ve emerged with one feeling:

I feel extremely powerful.

Let’s define power as the speed at which you turn an idea in your head into something in the real world.

- Less power = longer time from idea to reality.

- More power = shorter time from idea to reality.

With that definition, Astra has made me the most powerful I’ve ever been.

And this because of three main model improvements:

- It can operate tools it’s never been able to use before

- It can work with less concrete instruction than ever before

- It can stay on track for longer periods of time than ever before

Combine these three upgrades and you get something that wasn’t possible just a month ago: a model that can execute basically anything I do on a computer, often faster and more reliably than I can do myself.

For example, last week I was able to build a complete application funnel across 8 different software tools, in under an hour, all without leaving the ChatGPT desktop app, while I worked on something else.

And I’m seeing other people with absurd use cases like editing entire short-form and long-form videos inside the most advanced tools, without them having to do a single click.

And this new capability has forced me to confront a simple question:

If doing things can now happen this quickly, where should I actually spend my time?

And the answer to that question is what I want to talk about today.

1. What’s changed with the Astra release that makes it better than any other model

# What’s Changed With Astra

Using Astra inside Codex is the first “oh wow” moment I’ve had with AI since Claude Opus 4.6 came out about 7 months ago.

And there are three upgrades that led to this reaction:

1. Increased ability to use the browser & your computer

2. Increased ability to turn vague instructions into a clear plan

3. Increased ability to stay on track for extended periods of sequential tasks

I noticed each of these upgrades intuitively through my usage, and turns out OpenAI confirmed each of these individually in their [latest launch announcement](https://openai.com/index/gpt-6-astra/) (which I recommend you read as well).

Let’s take a closer look at each one.

One quick note. So far I’ve used Astra exclusively inside Codex (ChatGPT’s agent/coding work/browser work harness). It’s become my default tool for all AI usage, given a huge chunk of my work spans across multiple tools. If you’ve never used Codex, I recommend giving it a try when you tackle your next project.

## Better At Turning Vague Instructions Into A Clear Plan

Pulling directly from their announcement:

> When instructions leave room for interpretation, GPT‑6 Astra is better than previous models at making the right call. It uses context to fill in routine gaps and asks focused questions when the answer could change the outcome. In Codex, it can ask asynchronously while continuing work that doesn’t depend on your reply. If you don’t respond, it proceeds with sensible assumptions where appropriate, but waits for your input on consequential decisions.

With this update, I now start every new piece of work with the same steps in Codex:

1. Pop open voice mode

2. Brain dump what I’m trying to accomplish

3. Ask it to pull in relevant context on this from Slack, Fathom, Gmail, and Notion (or link to those directly if I have the links handy)

4. Finish with asking it to propose a build order for me to review and ask any clarifying questions it needs to understand the assignment

This has dramatically reduced the “activation energy” required for me to tackle anything I need to work on.

And as a result, I procrastinate less and stay in flow for much longer periods of time.

Think about the project that you’ve kept pushing to next week because it felt too intimidating to start.

This process completely eliminates that friction.

## Stays On Task & Keeps Itself Organized For Longer

Not only is Astra better at helping me start tasks, it’s better at keeping track of everything that gets done along the way, without getting derailed.

According to their release announcement:

> Astra is also better at staying oriented as a task evolves. Earlier models sometimes treated steering messages as a new goal, losing track of the original request or earlier constraints. Astra incorporates new requirements, changes course when asked, and answers side questions without dropping the broader task.

I’ve experienced this upgrade in two ways.

First, I can provide “steering” directions the second they pop up in my head, without it getting derailed from its original goal. I tend to operate less effectively if I have open loops swirling around, so this feature lets me immediately share them so I can move on to the next item.

Second, I spend almost no time documenting what’s been done as I mow through a project build. Instead, I simply wait until I need a recap or want to take a break and ask: “Recap all the progress we’ve made in a concise list of bullets, linking to any new relevant resource or built element, then give me the 3 next steps that come next.”

This lets me quickly transition out of a work session when I’m done, and back in when I’m ready to pick things back up.

The combination of its ability to “do” more things, turn brain dumps into organized plans, and organize along the way leads to one outcome:

I spend way less time managing work, and way more time moving it forward.

# How Astra Updated My Mental Model For Work

When I first started integrating AI into my business, I would (mistakenly) look at a project and ask: “can AI do this?”

- Can AI write tweets for me?

- Can AI write this newsletter?

- Can AI review sales calls for me?

- Can AI build this financial model?

- Can AI write client update reports?

- Etc. etc.

The problem is, those are projects. And asking “can AI do this entire project?” is not the right question to ask, because the answer is usually no.

So rather than asking whether or not AI can do this, I now start with how does AI integrate into this?

That forces me to break the project down into smaller tasks.

And once broken down, each task tends to fall into one of two buckets:

1. Decision Work. This is figuring out what to do and what you want to happen.

2. Doing Work. This is using technology to carry out what has been decided.

With Decision Work, I use AI to explore options, stress-test my thinking, and get clear on what I want.

With Doing Work, I give AI the desired result and have it fully carry out the execution.

Each new model release has shifted more of my time from doing to deciding. And this model release is the biggest “leap” in terms of what types of Doing Work can now be fully executed by AI.

For example, let’s take a look at deploying a new Typeform to a website.

The wrong question to ask would be “Can AI build this Typeform for me?”

Building a Typeform is a full project. There are many small subtasks within it, that we should define up front:

- Decide the questions in the form

- Decide which questions are required

- Load the questions into Typeform

- Update the branding of the form to match what you want

- Test the routing/paths of the form

- Copy/paste the embed code

- Paste it into your HTML

- Review the form to make sure it looks how you want it

From there, you can bucket the decisions into Deciding versus Doing.

Deciding:

- Decide the questions in the form

- Decide which questions are required

- Review the form to make sure it looks how you want it

Doing:

- Load the questions into Typeform

- Update the branding of the form to match what you want

- Test the routing/paths of the form

- Copy/paste the embed code

- Paste it into your HTML

Just a few months ago, you would have to do all the Deciding Work, and then the majority of the Doing Work.

But now Astra can execute every single task on this Doing list on its own more efficiently and more reliably than you can, as long as you decide the requirements.

You can run through this exercise for all types of projects, and you’ll quickly notice the difference between Deciding versus Doing.

For another example, let’s look at publishing a newsletter.

You need to decide the topic to cover, what to say, how to say it, what stories to include, how you want the banner to look, and what you want the reader to do next. AI can assist you with each of those to get to a better result.

Then, it can fully handle the uploading, scheduling, formatting, typo checks, graphic creation, and copy-paste distribution to other platforms.

I could walk through dozens more examples, but the point remains the same (and that’s why I’ve included a prompt below for you to run through this framework yourself).

In each case, AI can help you make the decisions, too. Once those decisions are made, you can delegate the work of carrying them out.

With this updated mental model explained, let’s look at an example of how I put it into practice.

# Full Walkthrough of How I Use Astra

Astra was able to build out a fully-connected funnel for our [Vortex Mastermind](http://vortexmastermind.com/) in under an hour.

- It fully built the landing page

- It fully assembled the Typeform

- It fully assembled the CRM system

- It fully built all of the email automations

- And it fully connected all the pieces with Zapier

All with zero Doing Work by me.

This project sat unfinished on my “Later List” for a few weeks, because I knew it would be a tedious build connecting 5+ tools with lots of minor testing & lots of button clicking. But once I saw Astra’s capabilities, I used this website build as a test to find out its full technical abilities.

So here’s the process I took:

- Step 1: Write down my first pass at what I wanted complete

- Step 2: Grant it access to any tools it needs to complete the build

- Step 3: Review its V1, give feedback and make another set of decisions

- Step 4: Repeat and iterate in plain English until I was happy with the result

- Step 5: Run a full test of the infrastructure and summarize what was finished

This is now the exact “loop” I take when I need to build anything with AI, so let’s walk through it.

## Step 1: Write down my first pass at what I wanted complete

The goal was to build a landing page for our Vortex Mastermind that did the following things:

- Clearly explained the offer, who it’s for, what they get, how to apply

- Embedded an application Typeform that asked them questions about their business

- Piped those Typeform responses into a lightweight Airtable CRM

- Notified me in Slack whenever someone new applied so I could review the application

- Hosted the website via GitHub & Vercel so we could make changes

- Hooked up the custom [vortexmastermind.com](http://vortexmastermind.com/) domain so we have a nice URL

This sounds like a relatively straightforward build, but there is a ton of tedious “doing” work that I knew would take up at least a day of focus to fully set up.

But when you break it down using the Deciding versus Doing framework, there’s a big difference between the amount of Deciding Work (only a few small decisions) versus the amount of Doing Work (tons of buttons to click to make it happen).

So from this bulleted write up, I’m able to drop it into Astra and let it drive the doing, only stopping when it needs me to make a decision.

Importantly, this does not have to be “perfect” - it just needs to be enough to have things moving in the right direction, as that progress is what will reveal the next decisions you need to make.

## Step 2: Grant it access to any tools it needs to complete the build

Once the rough build order is confirmed, you need to connect each of your tools.

You will connect every tool in one of two ways:

1. MCP plugins. If the tool you’re using has an MCP plugin, then Astra can use the tool directly inside Codex without any external browser usage.

2. Browser login. If it doesn’t have an MCP, then you need to log in via the in-app browser so it can do what it needs to do over there

For this build, we needed to connect 8 different tools.

1. Typeform - browser login.

2. Airtable - MCP login.

3. GitHub - MCP login.

4. Vercel - MCP login.

5. Namecheap - Browser login.

6. Slack - MCP login.

7. Calendly - browser login.

8. Zapier - Browser login.

Once I had those all connected, Astra had everything it needed to start building.

## Step 3: Review its V1, give feedback and make another set of decisions

After about 15 minutes of work, I had a working first draft that I could react to. Had I tried to connect all of these pieces + execute the build on my own, it would have been upwards of 3-4 hours. But every tool it connected worked perfectly on the first try.

Then from this V1, I had a handful of things I wanted to upgrade:

- I asked it to shorten the landing page and make the offer section more concise

- I gave it the brand kit I wanted to use because I forgot to attach it the first time

- I specified the exact offer stack I wanted to use, which I also forgot to attach on the first build

- I wanted it to use a Typeform rather than an Airtable form

- I refined the Typeform questions it used because it had way too many in its survey

Importantly, each of these were pieces of Decision Work.

I needed to use my judgment to hone in exactly what I wanted across each of these areas.

Then, once those decisions were made, it was Astra’s job to go implement all of them.

## Step 4: Repeat and iterate in plain English until I was happy with the result

After it implemented each of my new decisions, I continued this process with small iterations a few more times.

I communicated each of these in plain English, then they were magically completed. I was able to refine the output without opening each tool and making the edits myself.

I told it to “make the hero section shorter and move the testimonial above the application form” - and 30 seconds later both were done across both the HTML and the live site.

After a couple more full passes, it looked and worked exactly how I wanted.

## Step 5: Run a full test of the infrastructure and summarize what was finished

Once all the pieces were connected, I had Astra run a full end-to-end test of every piece.

This included:

- Navigating to the landing page that was now hosted on vortexmastermind.com

- Filling out the application by answering all of the questions

- Booking a call on my Calendly at the end of the application

- Checking that the entry was piped correctly into Airtable

- Checking that the notification was correctly piped into Slack

Incredibly, every one of these connected tools worked on the first try, exactly how I wanted it to. Watching it operate the browser, answer the questions, enter “dickie+codextest@dickiebush.com” as its email was a pretty mind-blowing experience.

And just like that, every piece was connected and the [website was live](http://vortexmastermind.com/).

The craziest part about this build is I completed it in the background while I was working on something else. Given there were only a few additional decisions to make after I made the first build order, I knew it would not be a huge context switch to confirm a couple more small decisions.

It felt like I had an intern sitting beside me at my desk, hacking away on a project while I worked on something else. Then, when it was almost done and needed a yes or no on a few choices, they tapped me on the shoulder to ask, then got right back to work.

At the end, just to have full documentation on everything it had done, I asked for a bullet-point summary breaking down everything it just completed.

Wild.
