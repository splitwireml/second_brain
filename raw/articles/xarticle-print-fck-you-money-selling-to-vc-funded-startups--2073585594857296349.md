# print "f*ck you" money selling to VC-funded startups on linkedin...

print "f*ck you" money selling to VC-funded startups on linkedin...

funded startups are some of the best b2b buyers on linkedin.

this is because they just raised money, they're under pressure to grow fast, the decision maker is reachable directly, and they buy quickly.

point a good outbound system at the ones that just got funded and you make a ton of money lol...

---

## why funded startups are the buyer:

a startup that just raised has cash in the bank and a mandate from its investors to spend it on growth.

that removes the two things that kill most b2b deals at once

1. no budget 
2. no urgency

they have both, right after the round closes, and they know the clock is running on showing progress.

they also move fast.

a founder or an early exec makes a buying decision in literal DAYS, because there's no procurement maze and no committee standing between them and a yes.

the same deal that takes six months at an enterprise takes a week at a funded startup.

and they're reachable on linkedin, where founders are active and a lot of them run open profiles you can message for free.

the person who controls the budget is the same person reading the inbox, so there's no gatekeeper to get past.

---

## finding the ones that just raised:

funding is one of the easiest triggers to track.

funding announcement wires push new rounds over free rss feeds EVERY DAY,

and startup directories like y combinator's and databases like crunchbase list funded companies,

you can filter them by sector, stage, and how recently they raised.

you pull the companies that raised recently in your lane and build your list from the freshest rounds,

because the window right after a raise is when the budget is new and the pressure to deploy it is highest.

a company that raised this month is a FAR BETTER lead than one that raised two years ago and has already spent or allocated the money.

you can also stack signals,

a recent raise plus active hiring tells you the company is not just funded but already spending to grow,

which is the strongest version of the trigger.

---

## the linkedin play:

you reach the founders and early execs through open profiles, which costs you nothing per message,

and you open on the raise and the growth pressure that comes with it.

the round is public, so referencing it reads as relevant and not intrusive

then you make a small ask, and you let the conversation move toward the call from there.

you keep the message short and specific to the stage they're at, because a seed stage founder and a series B executive have very different problems

you run this across every recently funded company that fits your icp, so the system is always working the newest rounds the week they close.
