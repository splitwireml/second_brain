## the two assets

a one person business scales on two assets wired together, and on nothing else

the first asset is software that already wins at its job: Stripe has billing solved, your CRM has pipeline solved, your booking tool has scheduling solved, Meta has a decade of ad delivery baked in

that layer already exists, and the only job left in it is operating

the second asset is your business knowledge: who your client is, what you charge, what a good lead looks like, which requests get escalated, how your service actually gets delivered

the missing piece was always the operator in the middle, the one who knows your business AND runs the software every day, because that person used to be a hire

an AI worker is the connection between proven software and your business knowledge, and that connection used to be a hire

there are two ways to build that connection: out of the box inside the workspace you already use, or by hand, agent by agent

i'll give you the fastest path first... the custom build comes after, for the operators who want to own every part of it

## the custom build: what's under the hood, and when it's worth owning

the custom build buys you total control, your data on your disk, and a system no vendor can change under you... the out-of-the-box build buys you speed, zero maintenance, and an office your clients already understand

plenty of operators land on Viktor precisely because it is plug-and-play and the engineering below is a full project of its own... but the ideas below are worth stealing no matter which side you pick

the mistake that makes handmade setups fragile is one giant assistant with one enormous prompt, expected to do everything

the builds that hold up do the opposite: one agent, one job

a research agent that only researches, a writer that only writes... and a reviewer that is never the author, because an agent grading its own work approves it every time

each specialist is assembled from the same four parts:

- an identity file: who this agent is, its scope, and the things it must never do... durable, rarely edited

- its slice of the knowledge base: the business context this job needs, nothing more

- its memory: what it has learned doing this job, bounded on purpose

- a schedule and a gate: when it wakes, and which of its outputs wait for your yes

keep the identity separate from the memory, always

the identity file is stable so a passing fact can never rewrite who the agent is... the memory is where churn belongs

that separation is the difference between an agent that stays itself over months and one that drifts the first time it saves a bad note

## the knowledge base: your business brain

every specialist reads from one shared brain, and the strongest version is embarrassingly simple: a folder of markdown files

an Obsidian vault is the operator favorite here, because it is exactly that... text files on your disk, linked, readable by you and by every agent you run

structure it by what the business knows, one note per fact cluster:

- the offer: what you sell, pricing, what's included, what's out of scope

- the client: who buys, the verticals, what a qualified lead looks like

- the voice: how you write, words you never use, examples of your best work

- the playbooks: how a project runs, stage by stage, with the checklists

- the rulings: every decision you've made that should never be re-argued

the rulings note is the one nobody builds and everyone needs... every time you correct an agent, the correction gets one line there, and every agent reads it before working

that is how a correction becomes permanent instead of repeated

the knowledge base is the training... the agents are interchangeable, the vault is the business

and this idea transfers straight back to the fastest path: on Viktor, the pinned context message is this vault in miniature, and it deserves the same care

## memory: the three levels, and where to stop

memory is where new builders overspend, so here is the whole field in three levels... you move up only when your problem forces you

- level 1 - files: markdown the agent loads at startup, facts and procedures and preferences

- level 2 - a memory product: a dedicated store like mem0, retrieval by meaning... for when the material genuinely outgrows what fits in the agent's context

- level 3 - a graph: entities and facts with time windows, for when "what changed when" is the real question

most one person businesses never need to leave level 1

keep the always-loaded core small and bounded... the working agents cap their memory files at a few hundred lines and load everything else on demand, because memory that grows without a ceiling slowly crowds out the actual work

and the hard half of memory is forgetting: give entries a review date, sweep what didn't earn its place

when a fact changes, write the new version and keep the old one marked as replaced, so a bad belief can be traced back to its source

one production store logged ten thousand entries in a month and about two hundred were worth keeping... untended memory looks healthy from the outside while it fills with junk

## the operating rules: managing a staff that never pushes back

the lanes are the easy half... the skill that decides whether this scales is management, because this is a staff that never pushes back and never flags its own doubt

so the rules below are the counterweight

rule 1 - the brief carries the context: every brief names the deliverable and what done means... a lazy brief produces confident work in the wrong direction, and you will read every word of it

rule 2 - permissions by blast radius: routine internal work runs without asking, and anything that sends or spends waits for your yes... the day the strictness annoys you is the day it saves you

rule 3 - you review, on a schedule: two windows a day to clear the queue of drafts and approvals, because the lanes produce faster than you read

rule 4 - cap the bill before it teaches you: every AI worker meters usage one way or another... so before the first brief, set a hard monthly spend cap, and quote-before-execute goes in every context message

## scaling: when to add the next lane

scaling a one person business is one question asked before every addition: did the last lane produce this morning without a prompt from me

the projects lane passes by design... content, the week the drafts stop needing rewrites... outreach, when the confidence column stays honest without you re-checking the sites

then the sequence repeats: write the context, connect the software the lane runs on, brief the work, correct with rules attached, put the sends behind your yes

and your job description changes shape along the way, because the lanes do the producing and you do the deciding

the approval queue becomes the workday: drafts in, decisions out, and every decision writes a line in the rulings note that makes tomorrow's queue shorter
