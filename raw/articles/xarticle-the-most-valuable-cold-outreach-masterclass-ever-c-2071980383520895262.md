the most valuable cold outreach masterclass ever created

---

## chapter 1: infrastructure

cold outreach starts with a CLEAN DNS

never send from your main domain. buy dedicated sending domains (minimum 3-4 per campaign), age them for 14-30 days before use, and configure 2-3 google workspace or microsoft 365 inboxes per domain. this gives you 6-12 sending inboxes per campaign.

every sending domain needs three records configured in DNS:

- SPF: tells receiving servers which mail servers are authorised to send for your domain

- DKIM: adds a cryptographic signature that verifies the email wasn't tampered with

- DMARC: tells receiving servers what to do with emails that fail authentication

every new inbox runs on a dedicated warmup platform for minimum 30 days. warmup sends start at 3-5 per day and ramp to 30-40 over 30 days.

sending limits: 40-50 emails per inbox per day. never above this.

the result of getting this right: 99.2% inbox placement across our active campaigns.

## chapter 2: list building and targeting

define your ICP at the highest possible precision:

- industry (specific, not "b2b")

- company size (headcount range)

- geography

- decision-maker role (specific job title)

- buying signal (what has to be true about their current situation)

list sources:

cold list approach: purchased or scraped from professional databases filtered by ICP criteria. lowest intent, highest volume requirement.

intent-sourced approach: leads scraped from public signals. linkedin posts asking for your service, job boards showing companies hiring for roles you replace, forum posts describing your exact problem, funding announcements from companies with a growth mandate. our 11.4% average reply rate comes from this source type.

target bounce rate: under 1%.

---

## chapter 3: copy structure

the highest-performing structure we run is the bait-and-switch: the first email doesn't pitch the actual service. it offers something smaller. the prospect says yes to the small thing, we deliver it, then we transition to the commercial conversation naturally.

the email framework:

- line 1: something specific to their situation

- line 2: the front-end offer or the specific mechanism and result

- line 3: one number. one company type. one result.

- line 4: soft ask. "want me to send it over?"

length: 50-65 words. plain text. no HTML. no links. no unsubscribe footer in the first two emails.

---

## chapter 4: the follow-up sequence

every follow-up must introduce genuinely new information.

our standard sequence structure:

- email 1 (day 1): the front-end offer or the primary angle. 60 words max.

- linkedin DM (day 2): different register, more conversational.

- email 2 (day 5): completely different angle. no reference to email 1.

- linkedin follow-up (day 8): case study fragment. leave it incomplete.

- email 3 (day 12): direct ask for 20 minutes.

- email 4 (day 18): breakup. short. no guilt. leave the door open.

speed to reply is the single biggest variable in converting a positive reply into a booked call. target: reply within 60 seconds during business hours.

## chapter 5: deliverability maintenance

weekly diagnostic checklist:

- run deliverability seed test on every sending domain. target: 95%+ primary inbox placement.

- check open rate by inbox. below 35% triggers warmup-only mode for 5-7 days.

- review bounce rate. above 1.5% means the enrichment process has a problem.

- confirm warmup platform is running on all inboxes.

the 90-day domain rotation: sending domains that have been active for 90+ days are flagged for reputation review. degraded domains are retired and replaced.

## chapter 6: AI integration

an AI agent monitors public signals across linkedin posts, job boards, community forums, and funding databases in real time. it flags leads who have publicly broadcast a buying signal, enriches their contact data, and surfaces them for outreach priority.

four prompt templates, one per signal type, generate personalised first lines for every intent-sourced lead. 500 leads processed in under 10 minutes.

the AI pulls campaign data weekly, compares against benchmarks, flags anything outside normal range, and drafts a recommended action for each flag.

## chapter 7: the three failure modes

every underperforming outbound campaign fits into one of three categories.

failure mode 1: deliverability

symptoms: low open rate (under 35%), high bounce rate (over 2%).

fix: pause sends from flagged inboxes. run warmup-only for 5-7 days. reduce daily send volume.

failure mode 2: copy or offer

symptoms: healthy open rate (50%+) with low reply rate (under 2%).

fix: test a different angle. test a different offer. run 1-2 variable changes per test cycle.

failure mode 3: reply handling

symptoms: positive replies coming in but low conversion to booked calls.

fix: implement the AI reply workflow. soften the first reply. shorten the path to booking.
