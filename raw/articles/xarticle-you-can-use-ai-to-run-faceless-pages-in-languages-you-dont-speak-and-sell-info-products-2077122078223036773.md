"perdí el cliente" is i lost the client.

i can identify the problem category without understanding every word.

THE CURRENCY AND PRICING LAYER

a mexican freelancer earning 15,000 pesos a month will pay 400-600 pesos for a pricing guide the same way an american freelancer earning $3,000 a month will pay $37-57.

THE DIALECT LAYER

this is the thing i didn't anticipate and it matters.

spanish is not one language.

it's one language with significant regional variations that native speakers notice immediately.

a product written in spain spanish sounds slightly off to a mexican reader.

a product written in mexican spanish sounds slightly off to someone in argentina.

the vocabulary differences are minor but they're real and they affect trust.

a mexican says "computadora." a spaniard says "ordenador."

a mexican says "coche." a spaniard says "carro."

a latin american says "manejar." a spaniard says "conducir."

small differences. but if a product uses spain vocabulary and the buyer is in mexico they notice.

and noticing anything slightly off in a product they just paid for immediately erodes trust in everything else.

i made a decision early on to target latin american spanish specifically and avoid the spain market for now.

the latin american market is bigger, the digital product space is less developed, and the dialect is more consistent across the region than the spain/latin america divide.

all my products are written in neutral latin american spanish.

claude can do this with a simple instruction in the prompt.

"write in neutral latin american spanish that would feel natural to readers in mexico, colombia, argentina, and chile. avoid spain-specific vocabulary."

done.
