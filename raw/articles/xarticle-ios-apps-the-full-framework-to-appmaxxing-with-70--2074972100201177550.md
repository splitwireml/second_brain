# Step 1: Building Your Factory - 10 Things to Know

If you're non-technical, I think you actually have an advantage to building in 2026. I started with Cursor and Sonnet in 2024. It was a struggle. But with Fable, or Opus, or the latest Sonnet even, you can wire up virtually the entire process of shipping an app to iOS with agents.  The biggest headache is going to be getting your Developer account from Apple. Which, by the way, I recommend getting an LLC (or your territory's equivalent) from the start.

1. You'll need a Mac, Git, Xcode, Claude/Codex, RevenueCat/Superwall. And maybe AIProxy. All of these are easy to learn with YouTube and an agent of your choosing. You just need patience. If you can make it past this gate, you'll be well on your way to ripping your hair out the first time you try to set up Meta SDKs or an MMP. Just give your agent the docs.

2. You likely DON'T need Firebase/Supabase or auth of any kind. It adds friction. Your job across all of your products is to REDUCE friction and SHOW VALUE as early as possible. Authentication gets in the way of what the consumer wants. @rohandave_ once said that you're fighting for the consumer's attention while they're scrolling on the toilet. While he was mainly referencing that in regards to paid growth; even though app store search is a warmer lead, it's still easy to lose a potential customer if you hit them with some stupid shit early on.

3. You'll  need to bring your engineering hat and think through architecture that's maintainable and scalable. If you're truly non-technical like I once was, this should be a lot of iteration with Claude/GPT + YouTube + Courses.

4. You need to watch an interview @maks6361 - he is the Original Appmaxxer - you should parallel your architecture style to his. For his factory, he is able to edit many apps at once since they reuse components. This is how I scale mine. You don't have to sacrifice quality with quantity anymore. You can deliver a high number of products that deliver one to three main features each, extremely well. Product quality is more important than ever.  You should also listen to whatever @adamlyttleapps says, (while Max is the original appmaxxer, Adam is the king of indie iOS apps), even if he thinks his strategy is weaker today than it used to be. Because his strategy can still work... if you hunt for ALPHA! More on this in section #2.

5. Never repeat keywords in your title, subtitle, or keywords field on iOS. This is a guiding principle and the core of ASO.

6. If your primary distribution channels are going to be ASO/SEO/AEO - then you should structure title as follows '[GENERIC KEYWORD OR PHRASE] + [BRANDED TERM]' NEVER '[BRANDED TERM] + [GENERIC TERM]'.  The autocomplete works in your favor - in some cases, even if you think [GENERIC TERM] may be saturated! If you're going to rely on UGC / Ads - make sure you DO NOT FOLLOW THIS ADVICE. Your number one priority in that case should be ranking #1 for your BRANDED TERM.

7. THERE IS A LOT OF ALPHA ON X, THE EVERYTHING APP, BUT THERE ARE ALSO A LOT OF IDIOTS. Don't follow advice from every random person you see on here. I'm talking about alpha in the next section, but it's like foraging for mushrooms, if you don't know what you're looking for, you might poison yourself. I tend to just drown out any noise like OH MY GOD THIS APPLE UPDATE JUST KILLED ASO - because it's mostly just bullshit driven for impressions. You can keep a pulse and infer what Apple is changing by getting really in-sync with your own FIRST PARTY DATA. Which you will be building as you amass your factory. This is far more valuable than whatever somebody on X says, and this disclaimer even applies to me. I'm just some guy.

8. Automate and link everything. All the tools I mentioned in bullet number one can easily be controlled by Claude or Codex. You need to be using the App Store Connect API as SOON AS HUMANLY POSSIBLE.  I am not understating this. If you are appmaxxing, giving your agents the App Store Connect API will lead to you saving hours of time away from App Store Connect UI.

9. Take many shots on goal, but make sure they're the right shots. If you set up your factory correctly, you should be making decisions based on what the market demands, or building new markets in new spaces. As a beginner, you should only build ideas that are validated by Astro or your favorite keyword research tool. But once you get more experience - get creative. Some of my best performing apps are hyperniche and created their own market.

10. This is said every day on X, but don't forget to enroll in the Apple Small Business program and reduce your Apple Tax from 30% to 15%

What does this all look like in practice? Like this: You'll have an organizational Apple Developer Account. You'll have accounts on the services mentioned above. You'll have a folder on your computer and your agents will have full access to it. Your agents will be easy able to set you up for continuous improvement by batch editing your portfolio with your latest and greatest features than translate well to other apps, when appropriate. Your agent should have API access and be able to perform the following flows:

1) Ideate and perform desk research on the web

2) Validate that research with Astro or equivalent tool

3) Scaffold out the app from your template

4) Push that app's metadata to App Store Connect (optimized via the keyword tool and best practices from skills / your own personal alpha base).

5) Fill out any other ASC metadata that you can programmatically, like creating subscription products, etc. Pricing is a whole nother beast, I'll try lump some insights into the next section about ALPHA.

6) You should then be able to have your agent fully set up, via each platform's MCP/API, Superwall or RevenueCat. I use both. I prefer SW's paywalls and RC's analytics.

7) You should find a screenshot skill or roll your own (what I did) that allows you to capture screenshots on your device and then make them pretty and ASO-optimized (target keywords in your screenshots) with your own tool on localhost. This is pretty easy to set up these days with Claude/Codex.

8) Spend time polishing your product and have Claude/Codex pentest it with agents. Then, do manual smoke tests, especially if you're new. Iterate with your agent. Importantly, ensure your agents utilize the memory function and BUILD YOUR OWN HOUSE STYLE. This iterative process gets better with each app generated. It's easy to build an app now. It's not easy to build great products.

9) Have your agent cross reference Apple Review guidelines and ensure you're playing by the books.

10) Repeat until you get good.

# The Bottom Line

ASO remains a valid path to distribution in 2026 if you are willing to accept the tradeoff that it takes time to compound and is not as easy to scale. But on the other hand, you can maintain some seriously high margins. My portfolio can bring in the same profit as an Adsmaxxer with $10k MRR! I think that's awesome. 

I make about $5k a month currently. In a typical month, my expenses are as follows: Apple Tax $750, Claude Code $100, APIs/Services $100. So about $1k/month in normal expenses Recently, I've also been spending $500-$1000 on advertising because I want to try Adsmaxxing for a bit, but I suck at it. So if you back out my advertising costs (my growth up to this point has been pure ASO/SEO/AEO),  an iOS app portfolio that relies on built in distribution can exceed 70% margins before taxes.

At the end of the day, this depends on your goals. I'm not doing this to scale indefinitely. I'm doing this with a goal to either heavily supplement or eventually replace a 9-5. If you want to scale, you can disregard a lot of what I just said and follow the adsmaxxers. If you're lazy and love sidequests, this may be attractive you.

A few final random ASO tips I've gleaned and memorized from X, so you can too:

- Launch an app with a small ASA budget, it helps your initial keyword indexing - ensure search match is always off and you're using advanced

- Keywords in screenshots get indexed

- It's never worth copying someone else's app name to ride off the coattails of their ASO. Copying generic terms and positioning strategies are fine and expected, but if you copy somebody's brand or clone apps identically, you're an idiot. Stay in good graces with Apple

- Ratings are important, but don't put the request in onboarding anymore. Reserve it to a post-paywall positive event in the main app flow
